# Hero Copy Rules

This file defines the rules for writing every piece of copy in the hero section: headline, subheadline, CTA, trust/proof line, and micro-zone content. It also defines the anti-patterns to ban and how to apply tone.

---

## Part 1: Headline Rules

The headline is the most important line of copy on the page. It determines whether a visitor reads on or leaves. It has one job: immediately communicate who this is for and what outcome or benefit they get.

### Headline structure types

**Outcome headline** — leads with the result the visitor wants
- Pattern: `[Specific Result] for [Target Audience] in [Location/Context]`
- Example: "Your Driveway and Siding Cleaned Right the First Time — or We Come Back Free"
- Use when: the outcome is specific, desirable, and credibly deliverable

**Proof headline** — leads with an evidence statement
- Pattern: `[Specific Proof Claim] — [Business Name / Category]`
- Example: "83 Five-Star Reviews and Counting — Austin's Top-Rated Pressure Washing"
- Use when: high review volume or a strong quantified result is the primary differentiator

**Offer headline** — leads with the specific service or deliverable
- Pattern: `[Specific Service] for [Location] Homeowners / Businesses`
- Example: "Professional Pressure Washing for Austin Homeowners"
- Use when: the offer itself is clear and the brand register calls for directness over cleverness
- Note: the most conservative option — clear, functional, but least distinctive

**Challenge headline** — opens with the visitor's problem or fear
- Pattern: `Tired of [Problem]? [Promise Statement]`
- Example: "Tired of Overpaying for Mediocre Results? Here's What Real Clean Looks Like."
- Use when: the brand register is conversational, the audience has a specific recognizable frustration, and the tone supports directness

### Headline length rules
- **Hard maximum:** 12 words
- **Preferred range:** 6–10 words
- Never split the core claim across a headline and subheadline — the headline must stand alone
- Headlines that require punctuation support to parse (em dashes, colons) to stay under the limit are acceptable — headlines that require two readings to understand are not

### Headline specificity requirements
The following words and phrases are banned from headlines because they communicate nothing specific:
- "professional," "quality," "excellence," "trusted," "expert," "dedicated," "passionate," "committed"
- "your [city]'s best," "top-rated" (unless supported by a source above the fold), "industry-leading"
- Any adjective that describes the business's self-perception without providing visitor-relevant information

**Test:** Replace the business name with a competitor's name. If the headline still works, it is not specific enough.

## Part 2: Subheadline Rules

The subheadline has one job: add a second piece of information the headline did not carry. It must not repeat the headline in different words.

### What the subheadline must add (choose one):
- The specific geographic service area (if the headline did not include it)
- The process, approach, or method that makes the offer credible
- The next logical question a visitor would ask after reading the headline
- A specific proof statement that supports the headline claim
- A constraint or qualifier that makes the offer more credible ("Serving Cedar Park, Round Rock, Georgetown, and Austin since 2014")

### Subheadline rules
- **Hard maximum:** 20 words
- **Preferred range:** 10–16 words
- One sentence. Never two.
- Do not use the subheadline to introduce features — that belongs in the next section
- Do not use the subheadline as an extended headline — it must be a complement, not a continuation
- Approachable brand registers: subheadline may be conversational ("No pressure sales. No mess left behind. Just results.")
- Authoritative brand registers: subheadline must be declarative and calm ("Board-certified practitioners. Evidence-based protocols. Outcomes tracked at every visit.")

## Part 3: CTA Rules

### Primary CTA label rules
- **Always verb-first:** "Get a Free Quote," "Book Your Consultation," "See How It Works"
- **Specific over generic:** "Get a Free Roof Inspection" beats "Contact Us" every time
- **Action-clear:** The visitor knows exactly what happens when they click — a phone call, a form, a booking calendar
- **Length:** 2–5 words preferred; 6 words maximum
- Never use: "Submit," "Click Here," "Learn More," "Get Started" — these are category-generic and communicate nothing about the specific next step

**CTA label by context:**
| Business type | Preferred CTA label pattern |
|---|---|
| Home services (free quote) | "Get a Free Quote" / "Get Your Free Estimate" |
| Medical / clinical | "Book a Consultation" / "Request an Appointment" |
| Consultants / professional | "Schedule a Call" / "Book a Discovery Call" |
| Salons / wellness | "Book Online" / "Reserve Your Appointment" |
| Emergency / urgent services | "Call Now — Available 24/7" / "Get Help Today" |

### Single primary CTA rule
The hero section has one primary CTA. One. Not two buttons of equal weight.

**Secondary CTA:** permitted only when there is a genuine secondary conversion path that serves a different visitor intent. Examples:
- Primary: "Get a Free Quote" | Secondary: "See Our Work" (gallery link for research-stage visitors)
- Primary: "Book a Consultation" | Secondary: "View Pricing" (for businesses where price is a known barrier)

Secondary CTAs must be visually subordinate to the primary CTA. Never same size, same color, same weight.

### CTA placement
- Primary CTA must appear above the fold on desktop and mobile
- For split layouts: CTA belongs in the text column, below subheadline
- For centered layouts: CTA belongs centered, below subheadline
- Never bury CTA below proof content — proof supports the CTA, not the other way around

## Part 4: Trust/Proof Line Rules

A trust/proof line is a short text element that appears near the CTA or below the headline — not in the micro-zone. It is a brief, scannable credibility signal.

**Examples:**
- "★★★★★ Rated 4.9 by 83 Austin homeowners"
- "Licensed, insured, and locally owned since 2011"
- "Board-certified. Results tracked. No upselling."

### Rules
- Maximum 10 words
- Must reference a specific, verifiable signal — not a vague claim
- Appropriate for: approachable and mid-register brands
- Authoritative brands: use the micro-zone for credentials instead of an inline proof line — a credentials row reads as more intentional than a parenthetical trust claim

---

## Part 5: Micro-Zone Copy Rules

The micro-zone appears below the hero fold. It is one strip. It has one job.

**Review count strip:**
- Format: `★★★★★ [Rating] · [Count] [Platform] reviews`
- Example: `★★★★★ 4.9 · 83 Google reviews`
- Never round up or inflate. Never guess. Use the number from `01-discovery` or flag it for confirmation.

**Proof statement row:**
- 2–3 short statements, visually separated (divider, icon, or spacing)
- Each statement: maximum 5 words
- Must be factual — no invented statistics
- Example: "14 years in Austin · Licensed + Insured · Satisfaction guaranteed"

**Credentials row:**
- Named certifications, licenses, or affiliations only
- Format: "[Credential Name] — [Issuing Body or Description]"
- Do not list generic titles ("Certified Professional") — specify the certification

**Social proof snippet (single testimonial):**
- Maximum 2 sentences from a real named person
- Must include first name + last initial (or first name + city if last initial unavailable)
- Pull the most specific, outcome-describing testimonial from the testimonials data

## Part 6: Copy Anti-Patterns

The following patterns are banned from hero copy. If you produce any of these, revise before outputting.

**1. Hollow adjectives in headlines**
"Professional. Reliable. Trusted." These words describe how every business wishes to be perceived. They are not differentiators. Ban all three from hero copy unless attached to a specific proof claim ("Trusted by 83 Austin homeowners").

**2. Features-not-benefits in headlines**
"Fully Licensed and Insured Exterior Cleaning Services" — this describes inputs, not outcomes. Translate to what the visitor gets: "A Clean Property — Guaranteed, or We Return Free."

**3. AI-generated clichés**
Phrases that signal machine authorship and reduce trust:
- "Transform your [space/business/life]"
- "Elevate your [anything]"
- "Experience the difference"
- "Your [noun] deserves the best"
- "Unleash the potential"
- "Where quality meets [value]"
- "Your trusted partner in [category]"

**4. Fake urgency**
- "Limited spots available" (unless actually true and confirmed)
- "Book before it's too late"
- "Don't wait" (without a specific reason)

**5. Audience-less copy**
Headlines that could apply to any visitor. The target audience must be implied or explicit: "Austin Homeowners," "Cedar Park Businesses," "Expectant Mothers," "Growing Startups." If the headline works for everyone, it works for no one.

**6. Double-benefit overload**
Cramming two unrelated benefit claims into one headline: "Affordable, Fast, Professional Pressure Washing with 100% Satisfaction Guaranteed." Pick one. The others belong in the subheadline or next section.

**7. Question headlines without a strong follow-through**
"Looking for a Trusted Pressure Washer in Austin?" — this works only if the subheadline immediately delivers the answer with proof. If the follow-through is weak, use an outcome or proof headline instead.

## Part 7: Tone Application Rules

Apply the brand register from `05-brand-direction` to copy choices. These are not stylistic preferences — they are constraints.

| Register dimension | Approachable end | Authoritative end |
|---|---|---|
| Headline voice | Direct, conversational ("Your driveway, done right") | Declarative, precise ("Results measured. Standards documented.") |
| Subheadline | Can use comma-separated rhythm ("No mess. No stress. Just clean.") | Full sentence, no fragments |
| CTA label | Casual-friendly ("Get your free quote") | Action-formal ("Request a consultation") |
| Proof line | Warm and social ("83 happy Austin neighbors") | Credential-specific ("Board-certified, peer-reviewed protocols") |
| Banned at approachable | Cold clinical language, distant third-person | — |
| Banned at authoritative | Casual contractions in headlines, exclamation marks, emoji-adjacent enthusiasm | — |

**If brand register is mid-range:** Use the five personality dimensions from `05-brand-direction` to resolve specific decisions. Confidence level and formality level will resolve most copy-tone ambiguity.
