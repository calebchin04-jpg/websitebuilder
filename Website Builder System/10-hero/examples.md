# Examples

Two examples in document-comparison format. Example 1 is Marcus Pro Wash (approachable brand register, proof-first, split layout). Example 2 is Elara Skin Studio (authoritative brand register, authority-first, centered editorial layout). Both show upstream input summary → full 10-section hero package.

---

## Example 1 — Marcus Pro Wash: Approachable · Proof-First · Split Layout

### Upstream input summary
- **Business:** Pressure washing, soft washing, and deck cleaning — Austin, TX and 4 surrounding cities
- **Primary CTA:** Get a Free Quote → /contact
- **Primary differentiator:** 83 Google reviews, 4.9 rating; real local owner (Marcus), not a franchise
- **Target audience:** Austin-area homeowners who want the work done right without surprises
- **Trust barrier:** "Any company can claim they're good — how do I know these guys will actually show up and do the job?"
- **Brand register:** Approachable, confidence level: high, warmth: high, formality: low, energy: medium, authority: medium
- **Photography:** Marcus at work (confirmed) + cleaned driveway before/after (confirmed)
- **Wireframe:** Split layout specified, text left, hero image right, trust strip below fold

---

### HERO FEATURE PACKAGE
**Project:** Marcus Pro Wash
**Page:** Homepage
**Stage:** 10-hero — output complete
**Hero type:** Proof-First
**Layout:** A — Split
**Prior-stage quality:** Strong

---

#### Section 1 — Hero Strategy Summary

**Primary conversion job:**
This hero must make Austin-area homeowners immediately understand that Marcus Pro Wash is the most reviewed, most trusted local option — and prompt them to request a free quote.

**Primary trust barrier:**
The visitor is most likely to leave without converting because they've been burned by unreliable contractors before and aren't sure if the reviews are real.

**Brand register:** Approachable

**Hero type selected:** Proof-First
**Reason:** 83 verified Google reviews is Marcus's clearest differentiator against competitors. Leading with this proof claim immediately addresses the trust barrier. The founder photo reinforces that this is a real, local, named person — not an anonymous service company.

**Micro-zone decision:** Include
**Micro-zone type:** Review count strip
**Reason:** The hero carries a copy-based proof claim; the micro-zone adds visual social proof reinforcement with the star rating that confirms the claim.

---

#### Section 2 — Content Hierarchy

1. **First (0–2 seconds):** Real results, real reviews — Marcus Pro Wash is the trusted local choice
2. **Second (2–4 seconds):** Austin and surrounding cities, specific services, local owner
3. **Third (4–6 seconds):** Get a free quote — easy, no commitment

---

#### Section 3 — Final Recommended Copy

**Headline:**
> Austin's Most-Reviewed Pressure Washing — and We Back Every Job

**Subheadline:**
> Serving Cedar Park, Round Rock, Georgetown, and Austin since 2014 — pressure washing, soft washing, and deck cleaning you can count on.

**Primary CTA:**
Label: `Get a Free Quote`
Destination: `/contact`

**Secondary CTA:**
Label: `See Our Work`
Destination: `/gallery`
Justification: Research-stage visitors who need to see proof of quality before contacting. Visual evidence is a key conversion path for this business.

**Trust/proof line:**
> ★★★★★ Rated 4.9 by 83 Austin homeowners

**Micro-zone content:**
> ★★★★★ 4.9 · 83 Google Reviews · Locally Owned · Licensed & Insured

---

#### Section 4 — Backup Copy Options

**Backup A:**
> Your Driveway Done Right — or Marcus Comes Back Free

**Rationale:** Leads with the outcome and the guarantee rather than the review count. Works better if the guarantee becomes the primary differentiator. Slightly more personality-forward; stays within the approachable register.

**Backup B:**
> 83 Austin Families Trust Marcus Pro Wash. Here's Why.

**Rationale:** Proof headline that leads with specificity (83, Austin, Marcus) and creates a curiosity hook. Slightly longer and requires strong subheadline follow-through. Use if the client wants a more direct social-proof-led tone.

---

#### Section 5 — Layout Logic

**Layout type:** A — Split

**Layout rationale:**
Marcus is the differentiator — a real, local owner with a name and a face. A split layout puts Marcus's photo or a before/after visual in the right half where it provides immediate social trust without interfering with the headline hierarchy. The approachable brand register supports a human-visible, non-corporate layout.

**Media direction:**
- Subject: Marcus in work uniform on a job site — OR — driveway before/after side-by-side composition
- Composition: If founder photo: Marcus in foreground, recently cleaned surface visible behind. Eye contact or three-quarter view. If before/after: split image showing dirty driveway on left, same driveway after cleaning on right — with high contrast between states.
- Tone: Bright natural daylight, warm Texas sun color temperature, authentic — not studio lit
- Minimum dimensions: 1600×900px (desktop), 800×600px square crop centered on subject (mobile)
- File names: `marcus-at-work.jpg` or `driveway-before-after-hero.jpg`

**Text legibility solution:**
Text column has a distinct background (design system neutral-base color) — not placed over the image. Split layout keeps text and image in separate zones. No overlay required.

**Desktop layout description:**
55% left column (text): headline, subheadline, CTA row (primary + secondary), trust/proof line. 45% right column: image with rounded corner treatment if design system specifies radius. Max-width container, vertically centered.

---

#### Section 6 — Trust/Proof Logic

**Primary proof signal in hero:**
Star rating + review count in the trust/proof line, placed directly below the CTAs. This is the most direct response to the trust barrier ("how do I know these guys are good?").

**Proof placement:**
Below CTA — positions the star rating as a reason to believe after seeing the ask, reducing friction rather than demanding attention before it.

**Micro-zone structure:**
Single full-width strip below hero section. Dark navy background (primary color). Star glyphs (★★★★★) in warm amber accent. Rating, review count, "Locally Owned," "Licensed & Insured" separated by light dividers (·). Centered. Single line.

**What was excluded and why:**
Guarantee badge was considered for the hero but moved to a later section — including it above the fold risks cluttering the trust signal with too many competing claims. One proof signal inside the hero; the guarantee earns its own placement further down the page.

---

#### Section 7 — Mobile Behavior

**Layout at mobile:**
Text column stacks above image. Headline, subheadline, CTA row (primary button full-width, secondary as text link below), trust/proof line. Image below, cropped to 4:3 aspect ratio, full-width.

**Headline at mobile:**
- Size token: `text-3xl` on mobile, `text-4xl` on tablet, `text-5xl` on desktop
- Line wrap: Confirmed ≤ 3 lines at 375px based on word count

**CTA at mobile:**
- Primary: Full-width (`w-full`) — standard for approachable service brand
- Secondary: Full-width text link, centered, below primary button
- Both visible above fold on 375px screen

**Image/media at mobile:**
- Crop: 4:3 landscape crop centered on subject
- Aspect ratio: 4:3
- Full-width below the text stack

**Micro-zone at mobile:**
Single line, centered. If full text wraps at 375px, reduce to: `★★★★★ 4.9 · 83 Reviews · Locally Owned`

**Trust/proof line at mobile:**
Visible above fold — positioned between subheadline and primary CTA. Confirmed fits at 375px on one line.

#### Section 8 — Asset Requirements

| Asset | Description | Format | Minimum size | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero image (desktop) | Marcus in work uniform on job site, OR driveway before/after split | JPG | 1600×900px | Confirmed per discovery |
| Hero image (mobile crop) | Centered portrait crop of subject | JPG | 800×600px | Same source, specify crop |
| OG image | Homepage social share image | JPG | 1200×630px | Needed — create in Phase 4 |

**Photography notes:**
Prefer an image of Marcus at an actual job site over a before/after for the hero — the human face builds more immediate trust. The before/after is best used in a dedicated section lower on the page. If a high-quality founder photo is unavailable, use the before/after pair in the hero and escalate photography as a Phase 4 asset requirement.

**Fallback if assets unavailable:**
Switch to Layout D (type-only). Deep navy primary color background. White headline + warm-white subheadline. Trust line and CTA remain. Flag photography as required before Phase 4 Polish.

---

#### Section 9 — Implementation Notes

**Component:** `components/sections/Hero.tsx`
**Variant prop value:** `variant="home"`
**Data file:** `data/pages/home.ts` → `hero` object

**Component type:** Server
**Reason:** All hero content is static — no interactive state required. Image served via `next/image`. CTA button links are static routes.

**Key props:**
```typescript
{
  headline: "Austin's Most-Reviewed Pressure Washing — and We Back Every Job",
  subheadline: "Serving Cedar Park, Round Rock, Georgetown, and Austin since 2014...",
  cta: { label: "Get a Free Quote", href: "/contact" },
  secondaryCta: { label: "See Our Work", href: "/gallery" },
  trustLine: "★★★★★ Rated 4.9 by 83 Austin homeowners",
  image: {
    src: "/images/hero/marcus-at-work.jpg",
    alt: "Marcus pressure washing a driveway in Austin",
    mobileSrc: "/images/hero/marcus-at-work-mobile.jpg"
  },
  variant: "home"
}
```

**Micro-zone component:** `components/sections/TrustBar.tsx`
Data sourced from `data/site.ts` → `siteConfig.reviews.count`, `siteConfig.reviews.rating`

**Animation:** Fade-in on load for text column only. CSS opacity transition, 300ms. No scroll-trigger for above-fold content. Image: no animation.

**Accessibility notes:**
- Hero image: `alt="Marcus pressure washing a driveway in Austin"` (descriptive)
- Secondary CTA: `aria-label` not needed — label text is self-descriptive
- Star glyphs in trust line: `aria-label="4.9 out of 5 stars"` on the star character sequence

## Example 2 — Elara Skin Studio: Authoritative · Authority-First · Centered Editorial Layout

### Upstream input summary
- **Business:** Medical aesthetics clinic — injectables, laser, skin resurfacing — Austin, TX
- **Primary CTA:** Book a Consultation → /book
- **Primary differentiator:** All treatments performed by a board-certified dermatologist (not nurses or aestheticians), evidence-based protocols, outcomes tracked
- **Target audience:** Women 32–55 in Austin who have tried med-spa treatments before and want clinical precision, not a sales environment
- **Trust barrier:** "Most med spas use nurse injectors and push upsells — is this place actually different or just marketing the same thing differently?"
- **Brand register:** Authoritative, confidence level: high, warmth: medium-low, formality: high, energy: low, authority: very high
- **Photography:** Editorial clinical environment photography (confirmed) — no founder photo requested
- **Wireframe:** Centered layout, full-width background image, minimal text, credentials strip below

---

### HERO FEATURE PACKAGE
**Project:** Elara Skin Studio
**Page:** Homepage
**Stage:** 10-hero — output complete
**Hero type:** Authority-First
**Layout:** B — Centered
**Prior-stage quality:** Strong

---

#### Section 1 — Hero Strategy Summary

**Primary conversion job:**
This hero must make Austin women in the target demographic immediately understand that Elara is clinically distinct from typical med spas — operated by a board-certified dermatologist, not a sales-driven aesthetician practice — and prompt them to book a consultation.

**Primary trust barrier:**
The visitor is most likely to leave without converting because they've been oversold at other med spas and are skeptical that Elara's positioning is genuinely different rather than a marketing claim.

**Brand register:** Authoritative

**Hero type selected:** Authority-First
**Reason:** The credential differentiator (board-certified dermatologist, evidence-based protocols) is the single most important separation from competitors. Leading with this authority claim speaks directly to the skeptical, experienced target audience. A proof-first hero (reviews) would signal "popular" not "precise" — the wrong register for this brand.

**Micro-zone decision:** Include
**Micro-zone type:** Credentials row
**Reason:** Board certification, specific credentials, and professional affiliations need a structured, clearly presented display. A credentials row reads as intentional clinical transparency — not a casual trust badge.

---

#### Section 2 — Content Hierarchy

1. **First (0–2 seconds):** Clinical precision — this is medical-grade, not spa-grade
2. **Second (2–4 seconds):** Board-certified dermatologist, not a nurse injector or aesthetician
3. **Third (4–6 seconds):** Book a consultation — measured, no pressure

---

#### Section 3 — Final Recommended Copy

**Headline:**
> Dermatologist-Led Aesthetics. Precision You Can Verify.

**Subheadline:**
> Every treatment at Elara is performed by a board-certified dermatologist — not delegated. Evidence-based protocols, outcomes documented at every visit.

**Primary CTA:**
Label: `Book a Consultation`
Destination: `/book`

**Secondary CTA:** N/A
**Reason:** The authority-first hero and premium register do not benefit from a secondary escape path. The consultation is the only conversion goal; offering an alternate route dilutes the clarity.

**Trust/proof line:** N/A — trust signals go in the credentials micro-zone, not inline. An inline trust line at this brand register reads as compensatory rather than confident.

**Micro-zone content:**
> Board-Certified Dermatologist (ABMS) · Fellow, American Academy of Dermatology · Austin's Only Physician-Led Aesthetic Practice

---

#### Section 4 — Backup Copy Options

**Backup A:**
> Medical-Grade Results. Physician-Only Standards.

**Rationale:** More compressed, slightly more formal. Works if the client prefers an even more declarative two-clause structure. Less specific than the primary — does not use the word "dermatologist" which is the key credential. Use only if "Dermatologist-Led" reads as too clinical in user testing.

**Backup B:**
> The Difference Between an Aesthetic Practice and a Medical One.

**Rationale:** Challenge-type headline that draws a clear line between Elara and competitors. High confidence level, potentially polarizing. Use if positioning against the med-spa category is the explicit strategic priority and the client approves of the implied competitive framing.

---

#### Section 5 — Layout Logic

**Layout type:** B — Centered

**Layout rationale:**
A centered layout with a full-width clinical environment image creates an immediate atmospheric impression of precision and calm — the visual register the brand direction specifies. The authoritative brand register does not call for a human face or warmth signal in the hero; it calls for environment and standards. A split layout would introduce warmth through the human-face convention that would work against the brand's deliberately precise positioning.

**Media direction:**
- Subject: Interior of the Elara treatment room or consultation space — clean, minimal, clinical but elevated. No people in frame. No medical equipment that creates anxiety (no needles, no lasers visible). Surfaces, light, and texture only.
- Composition: Wide establishing shot, clear horizontal reading, minimal clutter. Natural daylight or high-quality clinical white lighting.
- Tone: Cool-neutral to slightly warm, high contrast, editorial cleanliness — not sterile hospital white, but precise. Think high-end dental or specialist clinic, not hotel or day spa.
- Minimum dimensions: 2400×1200px source for full-bleed (retina desktop), 800×1000px portrait crop for mobile
- File name: `elara-treatment-room.jpg`

**Text legibility solution:**
Semi-transparent dark overlay (40–50% opacity over the image) using a color derived from the design system's deep neutral. Text in design system neutral-base (off-white). Headline is the largest element on screen. Test at actual resolution before finalizing overlay opacity.

**Desktop layout description:**
Full-width viewport-height image with overlay. Text block centered horizontally, positioned in upper-center vertical third. Headline large (design system display size). Subheadline below in standard body-large. CTA button below — contained width (not full-width), solid primary color. All centered.

---

#### Section 6 — Trust/Proof Logic

**Primary proof signal in hero:**
The headline itself is the proof signal — "Dermatologist-Led" and "Precision You Can Verify" are credential claims embedded in the headline. This is intentional for the authority-first type: the authority claim IS the headline, not a supplementary element.

**Proof placement:**
Micro-zone — the formal credentials display belongs in a structured strip, not scattered inside the hero. Placing credentials in the hero proper (as badges, inline text, etc.) would fragment visual focus. The micro-zone gives credentials a dedicated, structured zone that reads as intentional transparency.

**Micro-zone structure:**
White/neutral strip on near-white background below the hero. Three credential items separated by light vertical dividers. Small, precise typography (label-size from design system). No icons — text only. Left-to-right reading order: certification → fellowship → positioning claim. Centered, full-width.

**What was excluded and why:**
Review count was considered for the micro-zone but excluded. Review volume ("47 reviews") is not the authority signal this brand needs — and a low absolute number relative to high-volume competitors would work against the brand. Outcomes tracking documentation (results data) is appropriate for a further-down-page section, not the hero micro-zone.

---

#### Section 7 — Mobile Behavior

**Layout at mobile:**
Image transitions to portrait crop (tall, centered on the room's main visual element). Overlay remains. Text block centered, reduced size. CTA full-width at mobile only — exception to the contained-width desktop treatment — because a narrow contained CTA on a 375px screen creates tap-target issues.

**Headline at mobile:**
- Size token: `text-2xl` on mobile, `text-3xl` on tablet, `text-4xl` on desktop (display size from design system — smaller than a service business hero, consistent with the minimal editorial register)
- Line wrap: Confirmed ≤ 3 lines at 375px. "Dermatologist-Led Aesthetics." wraps cleanly to 2 lines at 375px.

**CTA at mobile:**
- Width: Full-width (`w-full`) — exception to contained-width desktop rule for tap-target adequacy
- Position: Confirmed above fold on 375px screen after text reduction

**Image/media at mobile:**
- Crop: Portrait crop (3:4 aspect ratio), centered on the primary room feature
- Overlay: Retain at same opacity — test legibility at this crop and adjust if the cropped background is lighter than the full-width version
- Fallback: If portrait crop loses meaning, switch to design system primary deep neutral as solid background on mobile only

**Micro-zone at mobile:**
Three credentials will likely need to stack vertically on mobile (one per line, centered). Do not truncate credential names to fit horizontally — if they don't fit on one line, stack them. Two items with one divider acceptable if three items cause layout issues.

**Trust/proof line at mobile:**
N/A — no inline trust/proof line used in this hero.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero image (desktop) | Elara treatment room, editorial quality, wide horizontal | JPG | 2400×1200px | Confirmed — photography scheduled |

The photography must be actual Elara premises — not stock clinical imagery. The brand's entire trust claim rests on clinical authenticity. #### Section 9 — Implementation Notes

**Component:** `components/sections/Hero.tsx`
**Variant prop value:** `variant="home"`
**Data file:** `data/pages/home.ts` → `hero` object

**Component type:** Server
**Reason:** All content is static. Image served via `next/image`. No interactive state in the hero.

**Key props:**
```typescript
{
  headline: "Dermatologist-Led Aesthetics. Precision You Can Verify.",
  subheadline: "Every treatment at Elara is performed by a board-certified dermatologist — not delegated. Evidence-based protocols, outcomes documented at every visit.",
  cta: { label: "Book a Consultation", href: "/book" },
  secondaryCta: undefined,
  trustLine: undefined,
  image: {
    src: "/images/hero/elara-treatment-room.jpg",
    alt: "Elara Skin Studio treatment room — Austin, TX",
    mobileSrc: undefined  // use next/image sizes prop for responsive crop
  },
  overlay: true,
  variant: "home"
}
```

**Micro-zone component:** `components/sections/CredentialsRow.tsx`
Static data hardcoded in component (credentials do not change frequently) — or sourced from `data/site.ts` → `siteConfig.credentials` array if client will update.

**Animation:** None on hero text or image. No entrance animation. The design system specifies "still entry" for authoritative-register heroes. Micro-zone: no animation.

**Accessibility notes:**
- Hero image: `alt="Elara Skin Studio treatment room — Austin, TX"` (descriptive, not decorative)
- Overlay: implemented as a CSS pseudo-element or a sibling `<div>` — not as `background-color` on the `<Image>` wrapper, which can cause z-index issues
- CTA button: aria-label not needed — "Book a Consultation" is self-descriptive
