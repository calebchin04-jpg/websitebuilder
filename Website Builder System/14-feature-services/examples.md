# Services Feature Agent — Worked Examples

These examples show how the services agent produces output across different business types and complexity levels. Each example is abbreviated — it demonstrates the key decisions, not the full template output.

---

## Example 1 — Home Services Contractor (Pressure Washing)

**Business:** Peak Ridge Pressure Washing, Austin TX
**Services from discovery:** Residential driveway cleaning, house washing, roof soft wash, deck/patio cleaning, commercial cleaning, gutter cleaning
**Audience:** Homeowners, property managers, some commercial
**Brand register:** Approachable-mid — local, competent, not corporate

---

### Strategy Summary

**Clarity problem:** Six services listed at flat weight with no hierarchy. Residential homeowner needs to quickly identify the right service; commercial and residential audiences are mixed without routing.

**Solution:** Group into two clear categories — "Residential" (driveway, house wash, roof, deck) and "Commercial" (commercial cleaning). Elevate house washing and driveway cleaning as primary visible services since they drive the most search and inquiry volume. Gutter cleaning is secondary.

**Depth level:** Light-to-moderate. Services are easy to understand by name. Moderate depth only for soft wash (commonly misunderstood vs. pressure washing) and commercial (different audience path needed).

---

### Service Inventory and Classification

| Service | Classification | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| House washing | Primary | Highest search volume; lead with this |
| Driveway/concrete cleaning | Primary | Second most common inquiry |
| Roof soft wash | Primary | Often confused with pressure washing — needs a brief clarification note |
| Deck and patio cleaning | Secondary | Companion to driveway; lower standalone inquiry |
| Gutter cleaning | Secondary | Frequently added to other jobs; mention as add-on |
| Commercial cleaning | Secondary | Different audience; route separately |

---

### Hierarchy Decision

1. House Washing and Driveway Cleaning lead the homepage services section at equal weight (both are primary)
2. Roof Soft Wash appears third — primary but slightly lower because it requires explanation
3. Deck/Patio and Gutter Cleaning as secondary items at reduced visual weight
4. Commercial gets its own row or a separate "For property managers" link — not mixed into the residential grid

---

### Homepage Layout Recommendation

**Layout D — Featured + Secondary** is not quite right here because there is no single dominant service. Use **Layout A — Card Grid** with 3 columns:
- Row 1: House Washing, Driveway Cleaning, Roof Soft Wash (primary — full card treatment)
- Row 2 (lighter weight): Deck & Patio, Gutter Cleaning (secondary — compact list or smaller cards)
- Below grid: "Commercial properties — [link]" as a brief standalone line, not a card

---

### Pricing Recommendation

**Starting-at pricing for homepage cards.** Most pressure washing work is scoped by linear footage, square footage, or property size — but pricing anchors help set expectations and filter inquiries.

- House Washing: "Starting at $[X] for homes up to [sq ft]"
- Driveway Cleaning: "Starting at $[X] for standard two-car driveway"
- Roof Soft Wash: "Starting at $[X] — final price based on roof size and condition"
- Deck/Patio: "Starting at $[X]"
- Gutter Cleaning: "Add-on from $[X]"
- Commercial: "Custom quote — contact for commercial properties"

**Assumption:** These prices need to be confirmed with the client before publishing.

---

### Key Copy Example — Roof Soft Wash Card

```
Name: Roof Soft Wash
Description: Low-pressure cleaning that removes algae, moss, and black streaks from shingles 
without the damage that high-pressure washing can cause. Safe for asphalt, tile, and metal roofs.
CTA: Get a quote
Proof note: Manufacturer-approved method
```

This card does real work: it explains the differentiator (soft = low pressure = safe), names the specific problems it solves (algae, moss, streaks), and addresses the trust barrier (will this damage my roof?) without being a wall of text.

## Example 2 — Medical Aesthetic Clinic

**Business:** Revive Aesthetic Studio, [City]
**Services from discovery:** Botox/Dysport, dermal fillers, chemical peels, laser hair removal, microneedling, IV drip therapy, lash services
**Audience:** Women 28–55, some men; self-pay; high-consideration decision
**Brand register:** Authoritative-premium — calm confidence, clinical but not cold

---

### Strategy Summary

**Clarity problem:** Seven services across very different categories (injectables, skin treatments, wellness, beauty). Visitors arrive knowing roughly what they want but need to confirm this clinic offers it and is qualified to do it. The challenge is trust and authority, not explanation volume.

**Solution:** Group into three clear service categories — "Injectables," "Skin Treatments," and "Wellness & Enhancements." Each category gets a brief framing statement. Individual service pages go deeper for injectables (highest trust barrier) and medium depth for the rest.

**Depth level:** Deep for injectables (Botox, fillers) — high-consideration, high-cost, trust-sensitive. Moderate for skin treatments. Light for lash services and IV therapy (lower barrier, easy to understand).

---

### Service Inventory and Classification

| Service | Classification | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Botox / Dysport | Primary | Highest search volume; core revenue driver |
| Dermal fillers | Primary | Co-equal with Botox; often same visit |
| Microneedling | Primary | Popular standalone treatment |
| Laser hair removal | Primary | Package-oriented; repeat visits |
| Chemical peels | Secondary | Often paired with other treatments |
| IV drip therapy | Secondary | Growing category but not core |
| Lash services | Supporting | Lower fit with premium clinical positioning; mention but do not lead |

---

### Hierarchy Decision

Injectables must lead. The homepage services section should not present lash services at the same visual weight as Botox — the audience for lash services is broader and less aligned with the premium clinical positioning.

Recommended homepage services: Botox/Dysport, Dermal Fillers, Microneedling, Laser Hair Removal (4 primary cards). Chemical Peels and IV Therapy as compact secondary items. Lash services: footer mention or "additional services" link only.

---

### Pricing Recommendation

**No hard pricing on the homepage.** For injectables, pricing depends on units and area — a flat price would mislead. For laser hair removal, package-based pricing makes more sense.

- Injectables: "Pricing based on treatment plan — schedule a consultation"
- Microneedling: "Starting at $[X] per session / [X]-session packages available"
- Laser hair removal: "Packages from $[X] — pricing by area"
- Chemical peels: "Starting at $[X]"
- IV therapy: "Starting at $[X] per session"

Consultation-first framing for injectables is important for this register — it signals expertise and appropriate care, not just commerce.

---

### Key Copy Example — Botox Service Card

```
Name: Botox & Dysport
Description: Injectable treatment for forehead lines, crow's feet, and frown lines. 
Results in 3–7 days, lasting 3–4 months. Administered by a licensed provider in a 30-minute visit.
CTA: Schedule a consultation
Proof note: Performed by [credential] — not delegated to less-trained staff
```

This card avoids vague language, gives the visitor a concrete picture (timeline, duration, visit length), and addresses the most common trust concern for injectables (who is actually doing this) without being defensive about it.

## Example 3 — Consultant (Business / Fractional CFO)

**Business:** Solo fractional CFO practice, B2B clients
**Services from discovery:** Monthly bookkeeping, financial reporting, cash flow management, fundraising support, CFO advisory retainer, one-time financial audits
**Audience:** Small business owners $1M–$10M revenue, startup founders raising their first round
**Brand register:** Authoritative — calm, clear, precise. Not warm and approachable.

---

### Strategy Summary

**Clarity problem:** Services range from operational (bookkeeping) to strategic (fundraising support, advisory retainer). Mixing them at equal weight creates confusion about what kind of firm this is. The business is strongest as a strategic CFO partner — bookkeeping should be clearly subordinate.

**Solution:** Separate into two tiers. Lead with CFO Advisory Retainer and Cash Flow / Financial Reporting as the core strategic offer. Treat Bookkeeping and Audits as supporting services that complement the advisory relationship.

**Depth level:** Moderate-to-deep for CFO Advisory and Fundraising Support — these are high-consideration, high-cost, relationship-based services. Light for bookkeeping — commodity service, self-explanatory.

---

### Service Inventory and Classification

| Service | Classification | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| CFO Advisory Retainer | Primary | Core offer; this is the brand identity |
| Fundraising support | Primary | High-value, differentiated service |
| Cash flow management | Primary | High-concern for target audience |
| Financial reporting | Secondary | Companion to advisory; rarely standalone |
| One-time financial audit | Secondary | Entry-point service for new clients |
| Monthly bookkeeping | Supporting | Present but de-emphasized; commodity risk |

---

### Hierarchy Decision

Homepage: Lead with CFO Advisory Retainer prominently. Fundraising Support and Cash Flow as co-equal seconds. Financial Reporting and Audit as compact secondary mentions. Bookkeeping as a brief footer note or an "also available" line — not a card.

This hierarchy signals: "We are strategic partners first. We can also handle the operational side if needed."

If Bookkeeping is given card-level treatment equal to CFO Advisory, the firm risks being perceived as a bookkeeping shop that also does strategy — the opposite of the intended positioning.

---

### Layout Recommendation

**Layout D — Featured + Secondary** is right here.

Featured block (left/top): CFO Advisory Retainer — 3–4 sentence description, an outcome statement, and "Schedule a discovery call" CTA.

Secondary list (right/below, compact): Fundraising Support, Cash Flow Management, Financial Reporting — name + one-liner only.

Supporting line at bottom: "Also available: Monthly Bookkeeping and one-time Financial Reviews — [contact link]"

---

### Key Copy Example — CFO Advisory Retainer (Featured Block)

```
Headline: Fractional CFO for Growing Companies

Description: An ongoing financial partnership for founders and operators who need senior-level 
financial guidance without a full-time executive hire. Covers financial reporting, KPI tracking, 
board/investor communication, and strategic cash management on a monthly retainer.

Best for: Companies at $1M–$10M revenue that have outgrown their bookkeeper but are not ready 
for a full-time CFO.

CTA: Schedule a discovery call
```

This copy is specific about scope (what is included), audience (who it is for), and the moment in business growth when this service makes sense — without using any filler language.
