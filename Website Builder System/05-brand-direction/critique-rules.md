# Critique Rules

This file defines what brand-critical information must exist before brand direction work can begin, and how to evaluate the quality of prior-stage outputs.

---

## What This Skill Needs From Prior Stages

Search for the following across all available inputs (wireframe PRD, discovery brief, planning PRD):

### A. Business Identity
- Does the business have a clear name and primary offer?
- Is there any stated positioning — even vague?
- Is there a stated or inferable reason why someone would choose this business?

**Why it matters:** Without this, brand direction cannot define a promise or positioning. Every decision becomes a guess.

---

### B. Differentiation
- Is there anything stated or inferable that distinguishes this business from alternatives?
- Does it come from proof (reviews, results, technique), model (process, guarantee), or positioning (niche, audience, price tier)?

**Why it matters:** A brand without a differentiator defaults to generic trust-language that sounds like everyone else.

---

### C. Core Audience and Their State of Mind
- Is there a described audience with a specific situation, need, or fear?
- Is there any indication of what matters most to them when making a decision?

**Why it matters:** Brand personality, tone, and emotional impression goals are all calibrated to the audience. Without audience definition, any brand direction is untethered.

---

### D. Trust and Proof Assets
- Is there any indication of what proof exists — reviews, testimonials, certifications, results, years in business, guarantees?
- Is there a sense of how strong or weak the trust position is?

**Why it matters:** The proof strategy and trust hierarchy depend entirely on what actually exists. If there is nothing, the brand direction must account for that and build around a different trust anchor.

---

### E. Any Stated Brand Preferences
- Are there any words, feelings, reference sites, or style notes from discovery?
- Are there any explicit "must-not-be" statements (e.g., "don't want to look like everyone else in our industry")?

**Why it matters:** These are starting constraints for personality, tone, and what-the-brand-must-not-feel-like. If completely absent, the brand direction must infer from business type and audience.

---

### F. Business Goal and Primary CTA
- Is there a primary conversion action?
- Is the business goal clear (more calls, more bookings, more leads)?

**Why it matters:** Brand direction must support conversion, not just aesthetics. Tone, messaging direction, and the first-impression goal are all calibrated to what the site needs visitors to do.

---

### G. Business Type and Market Context
- What type of business is this? (home services, medical, salon, consultant, fitness, agency, etc.)
- Is there a local or geographic context?

**Why it matters:** Market context determines what "differentiation" means and what "trust" looks like for this audience. A local handyman and a B2B consultant need entirely different brand foundations.

---

## Critique Criteria

Run each of the following checks. Record findings with the category label.

### 1. Is there a usable audience definition?

**Pass:** A specific person type with a situation, need, or fear is described.
**Fail:** "Everyone," "local customers," "small businesses" — any answer too broad to make brand decisions around.

---

### 2. Is there any stated or inferable differentiator?

**Pass:** At least one specific, non-generic reason why someone would choose this business.
**Fail:** Only generic claims like "we care," "quality service," "best in the city" with no proof or specificity behind them.

---

### 3. Is there enough context to define personality and tone?

**Pass:** Some combination of audience description, visual references, style preferences, or tone notes exists.
**Fail:** No style notes at all, no audience emotional context, no indication of how the brand should feel.

---

### 4. Is there a trust baseline to work from?

**Pass:** Proof assets are described, even loosely (e.g., "47 Google reviews," "licensed and insured," "free quote guarantee").
**Fail:** Trust is described only abstractly ("we want to seem trustworthy") with no actual assets or plan.

---

### 5. Is there a primary conversion goal?

**Pass:** One specific CTA is defined (call, book, form, quote request).
**Fail:** No CTA, or multiple equal CTAs with no ranking.

---

### 6. Is the market context clear enough for positioning?

**Pass:** Business type, location (if local), and rough competitive landscape are known.
**Fail:** Business type is ambiguous, market is undefined, or no competitive context is available.

---

## Send-Back Threshold

Send prior materials back if **two or more** of the following are true:

1. Audience is completely undefined (no usable audience description exists anywhere)
2. No differentiator exists and none can be inferred from available context
3. No trust assets exist and no trust strategy is stated
4. Primary conversion goal is missing or genuinely ambiguous
5. Business type is unclear enough that market positioning cannot be inferred

A single weak criterion is handled with flags and assumptions — not a send-back. The threshold is: "I cannot build a coherent brand direction without this."

---

## Critique Category Labels

Use these in the critique section output:

- `[IDENTITY]` — Business identity or brand premise gap
- `[POSITIONING]` — Weak or missing competitive positioning
- `[DIFFERENTIATION]` — No meaningful differentiator found
- `[AUDIENCE]` — Audience definition too vague for brand decisions
- `[TRUST]` — Trust assets or strategy missing or abstract
- `[PROOF]` — No concrete proof assets identified
- `[TONE]` — No usable style direction or brand feeling input
- `[GOAL]` — Conversion goal missing or too vague
- `[CONFLICT]` — Two prior-stage decisions contradict each other
