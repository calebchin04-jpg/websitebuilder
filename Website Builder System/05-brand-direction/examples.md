# Examples

Two examples in document-comparison format. Example 1 is Marcus Pro Wash (strong, continuing the thread). Example 2 is a new business — a solo financial consultant — showing a weak/vague input, a send-back, and the resulting brand direction.

---

## Example 1 — Marcus Pro Wash: Strong prior context → Strong brand direction

**Prior context summary:**
- 47 Google reviews at 4.9. Homeowners describe Marcus specifically: technique, surface care, no damage.
- Differentiator: "Technique-first" — uses correct PSI and method for each surface type. Proven by reviews.
- Free re-clean guarantee.
- Audience: Austin homeowners 35–60, quality-focused, primary fear: surface damage.
- Primary CTA: Call Marcus.
- Visual direction from discovery: Real photography, no blue-gradient hero, no stock photos, Marcus on-site.

---

### Critique

> **Overall quality:** Strong.
>
> `[TONE]` Discovery notes contain visual references but no explicit tone direction. Inferable from audience and personality context — labeled as assumption.
>
> No send-back required.

---

### Abbreviated brand direction PRD

**Brand in one sentence:**
The technique-first pressure washing expert who protects your property and backs every job with a free re-clean guarantee.

**Brand promise:**
> "Your property, done right — or I come back and do it again. No argument."

**Positioning statement:**
> For Austin homeowners who won't risk surface damage, Marcus Pro Wash is the pressure washing service that treats your property with the care it deserves. Unlike lower-priced contractors, Marcus uses the right equipment and technique for every surface — and 47 customers have already verified it.

**Primary differentiator:**
Technique precision, verified by reviews that specifically describe what competitors' reviews never mention: *what Marcus did* and *what he protected*. This is not "good service" — it is a demonstrable method difference backed by specific evidence.

**Proof hierarchy:**
1. Google review count + rating (47 at 4.9) — third-party verified, scannable, immediately credible
2. Before/after photos — makes quality tangible without the visitor having to take anything on faith
3. Guarantee badge — closes undecided visitors by removing risk
4. Specific testimonial quotes — provides depth for engaged visitors who want confirmation

**Audience perception goals:**

*At first impression:* "This is not just a guy with a truck. This person is a real expert with real proof."
*During evaluation:* "He specifically knows about my surface type. Other guys might have damaged it. He won't."
*At conversion:* "I'm protected by the guarantee. This is the obvious choice."

**Emotional impression goals:**
- Primary: Confidence — the visitor should feel certain, not anxious.
- Secondary: Relief — they've been looking for someone careful and they've found that person.
- Avoid: Overwhelmed — the site should feel clear and easy to act on, not like a complex decision.

**Brand personality:**

| Dimension | Position | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Confidence | 7/10 | Assured without bragging — facts do the talking |
| Warmth | 6/10 | Genuine and approachable — real person, not corporate |
| Formality | 4/10 | Conversational — speaks like a skilled tradesperson, not a marketing department |
| Energy | 5/10 | Calm and steady — not urgent, not aggressive |
| Authority | 6/10 | Category expert — more than a generalist, not an institutional brand |

**Personality in one sentence:**
The calm, careful expert who has done this a thousand times and backs every job — not flashy, not corporate, just genuinely good at the work.

**Tone:**

IS: Direct, evidence-led, confident, specific.
IS NOT: Salesy, inflated, corporate, urgent in a pressure way.

How the brand speaks about the customer: Like an adult who is making a real decision about their property. Their concern is valid and Marcus takes it seriously.

How the brand speaks about itself: Through results and proof, not through self-praise. Let the reviews say "best in Austin." The brand just states what it does and backs it with evidence.

**Brand vocabulary:**

USE: "Your [surface]," "right technique," "correct pressure," "protected," "backed by," "guaranteed," "results," "before and after," "careful," "safe for [surface type]"

BANNED:
- "World-class pressure washing" — unverifiable, sounds fake for a one-person local service
- "We pride ourselves on quality" — passive, self-congratulatory
- "Professional team" — dishonest if it's Marcus alone
- "Cutting-edge technology" — jargon that means nothing in this context
- "Satisfaction guaranteed" — too vague; the specific guarantee is far more powerful

**Founder story strategy:**
Story role: Personal trust anchor. Marcus is the service. The story is what makes "technique-first" feel real — because he cares about the work enough to learn it properly.
Story direction: "Marcus started this business after [origin event]. He takes technique seriously because [personal reason that the audience can respect]. When you hire Marcus, you're hiring someone who has spent years learning exactly how to protect your [surface type]."
Length: 3–5 sentences on the About page. Not a homepage essay.

**What the brand must not feel like:**
- **Must not feel like a discount service:** Marcus charges more because he knows more. The brand must signal quality without apology.
- **Must not feel like a corporate franchise:** The personal "hire Marcus" angle is a trust asset. Depersonalizing it destroys the local premium.
- **Must not feel like every other contractor site:** The blue-gradient-hero-with-stock-photo aesthetic is what the audience has learned to distrust. This brand earns above-average pricing and must look intentional.
- **Must not feel like a generic AI-generated site:** Real Marcus, real jobs, real results. That's the whole positioning.

**Practical brand rules for 06-design-system:**
- The brand's confidence-without-aggression personality should be expressed through strong but restrained visual hierarchy — not decorative, not timid.
- Warmth without casualness: the visual treatment should feel real and considered. Real photography of Marcus and real work — not lifestyle stock, not generic business imagery.
- Proof must be visually prominent: the trust strategy depends on review count and before/after being in the visitor's sightline before the first CTA.
- Nothing that signals "templated contractor site" — no blue gradients, no clip-art icons, no anonymous stock faces.

**Handoff to 06-design-system:**

| Brand quality | Visual expression direction |
|---|---|
| Confident without aggression | Strong typographic hierarchy; weight contrast without excess size; clear layout without decorative noise |
| Warm but expert | Real photography — Marcus working, real results. Layout clean and readable, not playful |
| Proof-first trust | Review count and star rating positioned visually above any decorative element. Before/after prominent early in page hierarchy |
| Local without small-time | The site should feel as considered as any regional professional service — not like a one-page flyer |

## Example 2 — Vantage Advisory (Financial Consultant): Weak input → Send-back → Brand direction

**Business:** Vantage Advisory, solo financial consultant, Dallas TX
**Input from prior stages:**
> "We help small businesses with their finances. We want to look professional and modern. We have a few client testimonials. We want people to book a call."

---

### Critique

> **Overall quality:** Weak — send-back threshold met.
>
> `[AUDIENCE]` "Small businesses" is a category of 30 million companies in the US. Cannot define brand personality, positioning, or tone without knowing who specifically and what financial problem they have.
>
> `[DIFFERENTIATION]` No differentiator stated or inferable. "Professional and modern" describes every consultant's website aspiration. There is nothing here a competing consultant could not claim.
>
> `[POSITIONING]` No positioning exists. Cannot place this brand in the client's mind relative to alternatives.
>
> `[PROOF]` "A few client testimonials" — no specificity. Count, content, and whether they are named or anonymous all affect the proof strategy significantly.
>
> **Send-back threshold met: 3 of 5 brand-critical foundations are missing.**

---

### Send-back

```
SEND-BACK TO 04-WIREFRAME
Reason: Brand-critical foundations are missing. Cannot define 
meaningful brand direction without these.

Required fixes:

1. Audience: "Small businesses" is too broad. What specific 
   type of small business? What specific financial situation 
   brings them to you — tax confusion, growth financing, cash 
   flow chaos, financial reporting? Describe the last 3 clients 
   you worked with and what they actually needed.

2. Differentiator: What makes Vantage Advisory the right choice 
   over the 50 other financial consultants in Dallas? Is it a 
   specific industry niche? A specific service approach? A result 
   type you're known for? A pricing model? A personal background 
   that's directly relevant? Something must be different — 
   "professional and modern" is what every competitor aims for.

3. Proof clarity: How many testimonials exist? Are they from 
   named clients with specific results, or generic? This affects 
   the entire trust strategy.

Brand risks from current gaps:
- Without audience definition: brand direction will default to 
  generic consultant language that sounds like every competitor.
- Without differentiator: no positioning is possible. The 
  site will feel like wallpaper.
```

---

### After return — revised input

- Audience: Professional services firms (law firms, accounting firms, architects) with 5–20 employees. They have a good business but chaotic finances — they don't know their margins, they're reactive about cash, and they've outgrown their bookkeeper but don't need a CFO yet.
- Differentiator: "Fractional CFO-level thinking without the CFO price tag." James (the founder) spent 12 years as a CFO at two professional services firms before going independent.
- Proof: 8 testimonials, 4 are named and include specific results ("reduced accounts receivable lag by 40 days," "finally understood our margins").
- No Google reviews but LinkedIn profile has 17 recommendations.

---

### Abbreviated brand direction PRD (post-return)

**Brand in one sentence:**
The former professional-services CFO who gives growing firms CFO-level financial clarity at a fraction of the cost — without the full-time hire.

**Brand promise:**
> "You'll understand your business finances with clarity you've never had before — or we figure out why together."

**Positioning:**
> For 5–20 person professional services firms who've outgrown their bookkeeper but don't need a full-time CFO, Vantage Advisory delivers senior financial strategy without the six-figure overhead. Unlike generalist bookkeepers or large accounting firms, James brings CFO-level pattern recognition from 12 years inside firms just like yours.

**Primary differentiator:**
12 years as an in-house CFO at professional services firms. Not a generalist accountant. Not a large firm with junior associates assigned to the client. A person who has actually run the finances of the exact type of business they're serving.

**Proof hierarchy:**
1. Named client results with specific outcomes (most credible, most specific)
2. James's CFO background (12 years, named firms or firm types)
3. Testimonial quotes
4. LinkedIn recommendations (third-party verified)

**Brand personality:**

| Dimension | Position |
|---|---|
| Confidence | 7/10 — sure of the analysis, not arrogant about it |
| Warmth | 5/10 — collegial, not overly personal. These are business relationships. |
| Formality | 6/10 — professional but direct. Not stiff. |
| Energy | 4/10 — calm and methodical. Financial decisions are not made in excitement. |
| Authority | 8/10 — this is a credentials-first purchase. Authority matters more than warmth here. |

**Personality:** The trusted financial colleague who has seen this exact situation before — and knows exactly what to do next. Not a guru, not a vendor, not a salesperson. A thinking partner with hard-earned expertise.

**What the brand must not feel like:**
- **Must not feel like a life coach:** This is business finance. The tone is rigorous and analytical, not inspirational.
- **Must not feel like a startup pitch:** The audience is buying stability, expertise, and relief from financial chaos. "Disruptive" and "game-changing" are exactly the wrong register.
- **Must not feel like a corporate accounting firm:** The whole positioning is that James is not that. He's direct, accessible, and personally engaged — not a firm where you never speak to a senior person.
- **Must not feel like a freelancer with a cheap portfolio site:** The audience is trusting someone with their firm's financial health. The brand must feel as serious as that responsibility.

**Tone:**
IS: Precise, direct, collegial, evidence-backed.
IS NOT: Inspiring, hyped, jargon-heavy, life-coach-adjacent.

How the brand speaks about the client: As a business owner with a real operational challenge — not a "small business dreamer." Treats them as financially intelligent people who want clarity, not education.

How the brand speaks about itself: Through outcomes and backgrounds. "Here's what happened with clients like you" + "Here's why James understands this." Not "we believe in empowering small businesses."

**Practical brand rules for 06-design-system:**
- This brand requires a precision-professional visual register — polished but not corporate-cold. The feeling should be "the smart, reliable advisor" not "the big four accounting firm."
- Proof components (named results, credentials, testimonials) must be structurally prominent — these are the primary trust mechanism, not decoration.
- The brand personality is measured and deliberate — visual energy should match. No animation excess, no visual hype.
- Must not look like a generic consultant template or a financial industry brochure. Needs to feel like a person's brand, not a category brand.

**Handoff to 06-design-system:**

| Brand quality | Visual expression direction |
|---|---|
| Precision-professional | Restrained, structured layout. Clear typography hierarchy. No decorative noise. |
| Credibility-first | Proof (results, credentials, LinkedIn) must have visual prominence above any decorative element |
| Collegial authority | Warmth through clean, readable treatment — not corporate coldness, not startup casualness |
| Measured, not flashy | No motion excess. No oversized hero animations. Visual energy matches the tone: deliberate. |
