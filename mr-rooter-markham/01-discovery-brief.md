## 2. Business Context

Mr. Rooter Plumbing of Markham is a locally-owned franchise that has served Markham and York Region since 1995. The franchise's strongest assets are its longevity, 24/7 emergency availability, and a brand promise (Done Right Promise®) backed by a national guarantee. ## 4. Target Audience

**Primary audience — Emergency caller:**
A homeowner in Markham facing an active plumbing crisis: burst pipe, flooded basement, no hot water, backed-up sewer. They are stressed and moving fast. They are not comparison shopping — they are looking for a plumber who answers the phone, arrives quickly, and won't surprise them with a bill. The phone number needs to be unmissable. Trust must land in under 5 seconds. Speed signals (24/7, no overtime, been local since 1995) are the conversion levers.

**Secondary audience — Scheduled job shopper:**
A homeowner planning a non-urgent service: replacing a water heater, installing a backwater valve, or diagnosing a slow drain. They are comparing 2–3 local plumbers. They read reviews, check credentials, and look for pricing signals. They will use a form or call to request an estimate. Trust here is built through social proof, service specificity, and professionalism of presentation.

**What they are NOT:** Commercial property managers, developers, DIY plumbers, or out-of-region searchers.

---

## 5. Customer Pain Points

- **Fear of price gouging:** Plumbing calls in emergencies often feel like a blank check. Customers worry they'll be charged a fortune because they had no choice.
- **Plumbers who don't show up:** Unreliable contractors who quote, disappear, or arrive hours late — especially painful in an emergency.
- **Hidden charges and overtime rates:** Being told an emergency call costs double. Customers have been burned by this before.
- **No idea who to trust:** The local plumbing market is crowded with unlicensed contractors, franchise templates, and faceless 1-800 operations. Hard to know who is actually local, licensed, and accountable.
- **Hard to reach:** Calling a plumber at 2am and getting voicemail. Emergency customers need to know someone will answer.

## 12. Visual Direction

**Overall feel:** Clean, structured, and professional — reads like a trusted regional firm, not a franchise template. White space dominates. Red is used as a precise accent (CTAs, emergency signals, TrustBar) not a background color. Grid is tight and logical. Feels like a business that's been around for 30 years and doesn't need to shout.

**References provided:**
1. Direction: "Clean and professional (corporate trades)" — white space, structured grid, restrained use of red. No specific URL provided.

**Design principles extracted from references:**
- Red is an accent, not a background. Use it for CTAs, alert signals, and the TrustBar — not section backgrounds or large fills.
- White space is intentional. Sections breathe. No visual clutter or competing elements.
- Grid discipline: content aligns to a consistent column structure. No floating elements, no misaligned cards.
- Typography does the heavy lifting. Hierarchy via weight and size, not decoration.
- No "franchise template" patterns: no stock photo of smiling plumber in header, no clip-art wrench icons, no red-everywhere layouts.

**Banned styles / what to avoid:**
- Generic franchise template appearance (Mr. Rooter's current mrrooter.ca style)
- Cluttered layouts with too many competing colors or elements
- Clip-art or low-quality icons for services
- Stock photo clichés (smiling contractor in front of a white background)
- Neon or gradient color palettes
- Oversized rounded corners (pill buttons, bubbly cards)
- Cheap "contractor website" patterns: scrolling logo marquees, animated GIF badges, yellow/black color combos

**Color constraints:**
- Brand colors (established): Red #C41515, Dark #111827, White #FFFFFF, Light grey #F4F4F5, Yellow accent #F59E0B (for offer banner / emergency badge only)
- Forbidden colors: Bright orange, neon green, blue (too close to competitor plumbing brands), yellow as a primary surface

**Font constraints:**
- No brand fonts established independently
- Direction: Clean sans-serif — modern and legible; implies professionalism without decoration
- Not serif (too formal/editorial for a trades business)
- Recommended: Inter or similar geometric sans-serif

**Animation level:** Subtle
- Definition: Fade-in on scroll for section content only. No looping animations, no parallax, no hover animations on critical CTAs (they must feel stable and clickable at all times). Smooth scroll transitions permitted.

**Perceived quality and trust register:** 7/10 toward "premium-corporate" end of the spectrum — polished and structured, but with enough warmth (local since 1995, named testimonials, real phone number) to feel approachable. Not luxury. Not budget. Trusted trades firm.
