# Services Copy Rules

This file defines copy formats, length constraints, quality rules, and anti-patterns for all services-related copy: service cards, overview sections, individual service pages, comparison blocks, and pricing blocks.

---

## Part 1: Service Card Copy Format

A service card contains three or four elements. Apply strict length limits.

**Service name (required):**
- 1–5 words
- Use the most recognizable name the audience would use, not internal jargon
- Avoid generic category words as the full name ("Cleaning" → "House Cleaning" or "Deep Clean Service")
- Avoid superlatives in service names ("Premium Window Cleaning" — the word "premium" adds nothing)

**Service description (required):**
- 1–2 sentences maximum on a card
- Must answer: what does this service do, for whom, and/or what problem does it solve?
- Do not repeat the service name in the first word of the description
- Do not start with "We offer" or "Our team provides"
- Do not use filler phrases: "high-quality," "exceptional," "top-tier," "best-in-class," "professional-grade"
- The description must be specific enough that removing the service name would make it sound different from every other service card

**CTA or link (required):**
- 2–4 words
- Specific: "Get a quote," "See what's included," "Book a session" — not generic "Learn more" as the only option
- At least one path on the card should lead toward a conversion action (quote, call, booking), not only deeper content

**Proof or differentiator note (optional):**
- 1 short line only: a certification, a specific number, or a narrow outcome claim
- Only include if it is real, specific, and relevant to this service
- Do not add proof notes to every card — reserve for services where trust is a meaningful barrier

---

## Part 2: Service Overview Section Copy

When a services section on the homepage needs a header and intro before the cards:

**Section headline:**
- 3–8 words
- States what the section is about directly: "What We Do," "Services," "How We Can Help"
- Avoid clever section headlines that obscure what's in the section
- Avoid: "Explore Our Offerings," "The Solutions You Need," "Built for You"

**Section lead text (optional):**
- 1–2 sentences only
- Use when the service list needs framing — particularly when the business has a distinctive approach or scope worth naming up front
- Do not use section lead text to repeat what the service cards themselves will say
- Do not use it to write marketing copy about the business; use it to orient the visitor

## Part 3: Individual Service Page Copy

Service pages require more structure than cards. Follow this format.

**Page headline (H1):**
- The service name, optionally with a short qualifying phrase
- Examples: "Residential Window Cleaning," "Custom Kitchen Renovations," "Medical Weight Loss Program"
- Not a tagline — a clear, findable name
- 2–6 words

**Framing subheading:**
- 1–2 sentences beneath the H1 that state who the service is for and what they get
- This is not a marketing slogan — it is a clarity statement
- Example: "For homeowners in [City] who want spotless windows without the hassle. We handle single-story to three-story homes."

**Service definition block:**
- What the service is
- What problem it solves
- Who it is typically for
- What the customer gets or experiences as a result
- 2–4 short paragraphs or an equivalent structured format
- Do not use vague outcome language ("you'll love the result") — be specific about what changes for the customer

**Process section (when included):**
- 3–6 numbered or named steps
- Each step: step name (2–4 words) + one sentence description
- Focus on reducing anxiety about the unknown — what happens, in what order, and what the customer has to do (usually very little)
- Do not invent a fancy process name if the actual process is simple

**What is included block (when included):**
- Bullet list of specific deliverables or inclusions
- Do not use inflated language ("industry-leading equipment" = meaningless)
- Do not list obvious things as features ("our team will show up" is not a selling point)

**Proof block for this service (when included):**
- One specific testimonial, result, or case note tied to this specific service
- Not a generic "great company" review — a review that mentions this service and the outcome
- Format: quote → attribution (first name + last initial, location or job type)

**FAQ block (when included):**
- 3–6 questions the audience actually asks before booking this service
- Questions should reflect real objections, concerns, or confusion — not puff questions ("Is your company licensed?" is less useful than "How long does the service take and do I need to be home?")
- Answers: 2–4 sentences each; plain, direct language

**Related services (when included):**
- Short names only + one-line descriptions
- 2–4 related services maximum
- Do not write full cards — just enough to create a useful navigation path

## Part 4: Pricing Copy Rules

Pricing copy must be honest and calibrated to how the business actually works.

**Starting-at format:**
> Starting at $X for [brief scope description]
> Final pricing depends on [key variable — sq ft, project size, number of units, etc.]

- The scope description must be specific enough that the visitor knows what "$X" actually buys
- Never use "starting at $X" if the actual starting price is rarely available — the visitor will feel misled when they get a real quote

**Range format:**
> $X–$Y depending on [variable]

- Only use a range if the range is honest and not so wide it is meaningless
- "$100–$5,000" is not a useful range — it is not a pricing communication

**Package block format:**
> [Package name]: [What is included — 3–5 items max] — $X [or "starting at $X"]

- Package names should map to real audience situations, not marketing tiers ("Family Package" vs. "Gold Tier")
- Highlight the most commonly chosen or recommended package — do not hide it

**Quote-first format:**
> Every project is priced based on [key variable]. Request a free quote and we'll give you an exact number within [timeframe].

- This is honest when pricing is genuinely variable
- The follow-up action must be clear and easy — a quote form, a call, or a short intake form
- Do not use "contact us for pricing" with no further context — this reads as evasiveness

**Banned pricing copy:**
- Fake comparison pricing ("Normally $500, now $299") unless there is a real, current promotion
- Vague disclaimers that undermine the pricing entirely ("Prices vary significantly")
- Pricing tables that add fees in small print that were not mentioned in the headline price

## Part 5: Comparison Block Copy Rules

When a comparison block is used, the copy in each column or card must do real work.

**Column/card header:**
- Tier or option name: 1–3 words
- Avoid: "Basic," "Standard," "Premium" as the only differentiators — add a use-case qualifier ("For one room," "For the full home," "For recurring visits")

**Feature rows or bullet lists:**
- State what is included, not what is "unlocked"
- Use plain, specific language: "Monthly service visits" not "Ongoing support included"
- Checkmarks are acceptable for inclusion/exclusion — use em dashes for unavailable features, not red X marks (X marks feel like punishments for not buying the higher tier)

**Recommended tier callout:**
- Label it simply: "Most popular" or "Best for most homeowners"
- Do not use aggressive marketing labels ("Best value!" "Don't miss this!")

**Comparison block headline:**
- State what the visitor is comparing: "Choose the plan that fits your property" or "Find the right service level"
- Not: "Find Your Perfect Solution"

## Part 6: Copy Anti-Patterns

These patterns are banned from all services copy.

**1. Filler adjectives as differentiators**
"Professional," "experienced," "high-quality," "expert," "trusted," "reliable" — these are expected minimums, not differentiators. If a claim cannot be supported with evidence, it should not appear.

**2. We-first copy**
Starting descriptions with "We offer," "We provide," "Our team delivers" centers the business, not the customer. Rewrite to center what the customer gets or experiences.

**3. Vague outcome language**
"Results you'll love," "a difference you can see," "the best experience possible." These are promises without content. State the actual outcome: what changes, what the space looks like, how the visitor feels, what they no longer have to deal with.

**4. Services described identically**
When two different service cards or descriptions could be swapped and the reader would not notice, the descriptions are not doing their job. Every service must sound different because it is different.

**5. Category-level service names with no specificity**
"Cleaning Services," "Renovation Services," "Medical Services" — these are business categories, not service names. Get specific: "Deep House Clean," "Bathroom Renovation," "Weight Loss Program."

**6. Implied urgency that is not real**
"Limited availability," "Book before spots run out" — if this is not true right now for this specific service, do not use it. False urgency destroys trust faster than no urgency.

**7. Process copy that is actually marketing copy**
Step-by-step processes should describe what actually happens, not what sounds impressive. "Step 1: We assess your vision" is not a process step — it is marketing language dressed as information.

**8. FAQ questions no one asks**
"Why should I choose [Business Name]?" is not a question a customer asks before booking a service. FAQ blocks must reflect real questions and real hesitations, not give the business another opportunity to sell.

**9. Price anchoring without context**
Mentioning a competitor's higher price or a theoretical "full price" without clear, verifiable context. This reads as dishonest and undermines the credibility of the entire pricing section.
