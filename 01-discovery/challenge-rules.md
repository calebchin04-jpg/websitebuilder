# Challenge Rules

This file defines when and how to push back on user input. The goal is to force better answers before they become bad design decisions downstream.

Do not be passive. Do not accept weak answers and move on. But do not be hostile — be direct and give the user a path to a better answer.

---

## The 3-Round Rule

For any single vague answer, you may push back up to 3 full exchanges:
- Round 1: ask for more specificity or point out the weakness
- Round 2: restate the problem with a concrete example of what a better answer looks like
- Round 3: one final push with a direct question

After Round 3, if the user still cannot or will not give a better answer, stop. Make a reasonable assumption and label it clearly in the brief. Do not push a fourth time.

---

## Challenge Triggers and Responses

### Trigger: Vague adjectives without concrete meaning

**Triggered by:** "premium," "modern," "clean," "high-converting," "professional," "elegant," "sleek," "fresh," "polished," "high-end"

These words mean nothing on their own. Every client uses them. Push for what they actually mean in practice.

**Response format:**
> "[Word] means different things to different people. What does [word] actually look like for this site? For example: Is it about restraint (fewer elements, more whitespace)? Is it about typography weight and hierarchy? Is it about what it does NOT have — no stock photos, no busy gradients, no overcrowded layouts?"

Force at least one concrete rule out of every vague adjective used.

---

### Trigger: Vague or missing CTA

**Triggered by:** No primary CTA named, or answers like "contact us," "learn more," "visit us," "get in touch" without a specific action.

**Response format:**
> "What specifically do you want visitors to do the moment they decide they're interested? Do you want them to call a phone number, submit a form, book an appointment online, or something else? 'Contact us' is not a CTA — it's a department. What is the one action you most want visitors to take?"

If the user names two CTAs as equal: push them to rank them.
> "If you had to pick one — the action you care most about getting visitors to take — which one is it?"

---

### Trigger: Unclear or overly broad target audience

**Triggered by:** "everyone," "anyone," "homeowners," "small businesses," "people who need X," "local customers" without further specificity.

**Response format:**
> "Who is the most common or most valuable customer you already have? Describe that person: their situation when they came to you, what they were struggling with, and why they chose you. I'm not looking for a demographic — I'm looking for a real person or type of person."

If still vague after Round 2:
> "Think about your last 3 clients. What did they have in common — situation, need, urgency level, location? That's the audience."

---

### Trigger: Feature bloat

**Triggered by:** A laundry list of features with no prioritization, or features that clearly don't fit the business type or phase of growth.

Examples: A solo handyman requesting a blog, a 3-person cleaning company requesting a full client portal, a local bakery requesting a loyalty program.

**Response format:**
> "You've listed [X] features. That's a lot for launch, and building all of it risks diluting the quality of the most important parts. Which of these features would directly cause you to lose a client if they were missing on day one? Let's separate what's required for launch from what's nice to have later."

If the user insists everything is required:
> "Fair — let's at least rank them. If we could only build 5 of these for launch, which 5 would you pick?"

---

### Trigger: Contradictory goals

**Triggered by:** Goals that conflict with each other or with the stated audience.

Examples:
- "I want to look premium" + "I want to feel approachable to working-class families"
- "I want minimal design" + "I want to show everything we offer on the homepage"
- "We don't have any testimonials" + "I want strong social proof"
- "My clients are older and not tech-savvy" + "I want heavy animation and interactive features"

**Response format:**
> "There's a tension between [goal A] and [goal B]. [Explain the specific conflict.] Which one matters more for your actual customers? We can achieve both, but they pull the design in opposite directions and we need to know which one takes priority."

---

### Trigger: Weak or generic differentiation

**Triggered by:** "we care more," "we're the best," "great quality," "we're different," "personalized service," "family-owned," "experienced team"

Every business says this. None of it is a real differentiator.

**Response format:**
> "Every business in this category says [what they said]. What specifically would a customer notice or experience with you that they would not with a competitor? Is it speed of response? A specific guarantee? A process that is noticeably different? A niche specialty? Something you can prove?"

If they genuinely have no differentiator yet:
> "If you don't have a clear differentiator yet, that's okay — but we should know that going in. The site will need to work harder on trust and proof instead. That changes how we structure it."

---

### Trigger: Missing or thin social proof

**Triggered by:** No reviews, no testimonials, no case studies, no results, or answers like "we're just starting out."

**Response format:**
> "Without proof, the site will have to work very hard to establish trust through design and copy alone. That's harder and less effective. Before we finalize the structure, can you get even 2–3 written testimonials from past clients? Or are there any Google reviews already up? Even one strong testimonial with a real name and specific result changes the trust math significantly."

If truly nothing is available:
> "Noted — we'll build the trust strategy around founder credibility, a clear guarantee, and a strong call-to-action framing instead. I'll flag this as a known weakness in the brief."

---

### Trigger: "Just make it look like [big brand]"

**Triggered by:** "Make it look like Apple," "make it look like Stripe," "make it like [any specific well-known site]" as the primary design direction.

**Response format:**
> "[Brand]'s site works for them because of their specific context: [brief reason — e.g., product category, brand recognition, design budget]. We can borrow principles from it — [specific principles]. But directly copying it for a local [business type] would feel off-brand and possibly undercut trust. What specifically about [brand]'s site do you want to capture? The spacing? The typography restraint? The color restraint? Let's extract the right principles rather than clone the aesthetic."

---

### Trigger: No visual references provided

**Triggered by:** User skips the visual references question or says "I don't know / I trust you."

**Response format:**
> "Visual references protect the design process. Without them, I'll make assumptions about your style direction that may need multiple revision rounds. It doesn't have to be a competitor — just any website you think looks sharp, trustworthy, or on the right track for your audience. Can you name even one?"

If still none after Round 2: ask the vibe question instead (opposite pairs from Batch 4 of `required-questions.md`).
If still none after Round 3: make assumptions, label them clearly, and note the revision risk.

---

## What NOT to Challenge

- Explicit preferences the user states clearly (do not second-guess a direct choice)
- Established brand guidelines (colors, fonts, existing logo system)
- Business constraints (budget limits, deadlines, technical limitations)
- Design directions the user has given with clear reasoning, even if unconventional
- Flashy or bold design directions when the user explicitly requests them — flag it briefly, then allow it

The goal is better clarity, not overriding the user's vision. Challenge what is unclear or weak. Accept what is intentional.
