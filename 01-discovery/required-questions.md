# Required Questions

These questions must be answered before the output brief is produced. They are organized into batches matching the session flow in `SKILL.md`.

If a question is skipped or answered vaguely, apply challenge rules before accepting the answer or moving on.

---

## Batch 1 — Business and Offer

**1.1 Business name and type**
What is the business name? What does it do — what service or product does it sell?

**1.2 Primary service or offer**
What is the main thing they sell or do? Is it one service, multiple services, packages, or products?

If the answer is "everything" or a long list: push back. Ask them to name their most important or most profitable offer. That becomes the site's primary focus.

**1.3 Target audience**
Who is the ideal customer? Describe the person: what do they need, where are they, what's their situation when they come looking for this business?

Accept: a specific description of a real person or customer type.
Reject: "everyone," "anyone," "small businesses," "homeowners" — push for more specificity.

**1.4 Primary conversion action (primary CTA)**
What is the single most important thing a visitor should do on this site? Options: call, book appointment, request quote, fill out a form, buy, sign up.

There must be exactly one primary CTA. If the user names two or three, push them to rank them.

**1.5 Geographic scope**
Where does the business serve customers? Single city, metro area, multiple cities, statewide, national?

If local: ask which city or region is most important for SEO and trust signals.

## Batch 2 — Trust, Proof, and Positioning

**2.1 Trust signals available**
What proof does the business have? This can include: customer reviews (Google, Yelp, etc.), testimonials, certifications, licenses, years in business, number of clients served, notable results, before/after photos, awards, media mentions, partner logos.

If the answer is "nothing" or "not yet": flag this as a trust gap. Ask how they plan to build early trust (even founder story, guarantee, etc.).

**2.2 Customer pain points**
What is the customer's main problem or frustration before they find this business? What are they struggling with, worried about, or trying to avoid?

If vague: ask them to describe the last 3 customers and what problem brought each one in.

**2.3 Differentiation**
Why would someone choose this business over a competitor? What makes it different, better, or the right choice for the target customer?

If the answer is "quality" or "we care more" or "great service": challenge it. Ask what evidence backs that up and whether a competitor could say the same thing. Push until there is a real, specific differentiator.

**2.4 Known competitors**
Are there any local or direct competitors the client is aware of? Do any competitor sites do something right that they want to match or beat?

This is optional to answer fully, but if competitors are named, note their sites.

## Batch 3 — Site Structure and Content

**3.1 Pages needed**
What pages does the site need? Start with what the user says, then verify against the business type and offer structure.

Standard local business pages: Home, Services, About, Contact.
Suggest additions if relevant: Service Area, Gallery, Testimonials, FAQ, Blog, Booking, Pricing.

If the user lists many pages: push them to prioritize. Which 5 pages would be enough to get the first client?

**3.2 Content and assets available**
What does the agency already have for this client? Logo, brand colors, brand fonts, photos, copy, existing bio or story, previous marketing materials?

Mark what is available and what is missing. Missing content blocks must be flagged in the brief.

**3.3 Testimonials and reviews**
Are there real customer testimonials or public reviews available (Google, Yelp, etc.)? How many? Are they strong enough to use?

If no testimonials exist: flag as a trust gap. Ask if the client can collect 2–3 before launch.

**3.4 Secondary CTA**
Is there a secondary action the site should push for? Examples: newsletter signup, free consultation, download a resource, follow on social media.

If no secondary CTA is needed, mark as none. Do not force one.

## Batch 4 — Visual Direction

**4.1 Visual references**
Ask for 2–3 websites the client likes the look and feel of. These do not need to be competitors or even in the same industry.

If none are provided: ask them to describe the feeling they want the site to create in a visitor. What should the visitor think or feel in the first 5 seconds?

Do not skip this step. Visual direction with no reference is a risk to design quality.

**4.2 Preferred vibe / design feeling**
How should the site feel? Prompt the user with opposite pairs to force a decision:
- Local and personal vs. polished and corporate?
- Bold and confident vs. calm and reassuring?
- Clean and minimal vs. rich and detailed?
- Warm and approachable vs. cool and premium?

If they say "all of the above": challenge it. Ask them to pick the most important feeling for their audience.

**4.3 Banned styles**
Are there any visual styles, colors, layouts, or design patterns the client hates or wants to avoid?

Also ask: have they seen websites in their industry that look cheap, fake, or generic? What specifically bothers them about those sites?

If they mention specific AI-generated or template-looking sites as things to avoid: note that explicitly.

**4.4 Colors**
Does the business have existing brand colors? If yes, what are they?

If no brand colors exist: ask if they have a strong preference or association (e.g., "we want to feel trustworthy and calm, so blues and neutrals"). Note any colors that are off-limits.

Do not pick colors here. Collect constraints only.

**4.5 Fonts**
Does the client have existing brand fonts? If yes, what are they?

If none: ask if they lean toward serif (classic, trusted) or sans-serif (clean, modern). Note any strong preferences or aversions.

Do not pick fonts here. Collect constraints only.

**4.6 Animation level**
How much motion do they want on the site?
- None (static, stable, no movement)
- Subtle (fade-ins, smooth scroll transitions only)
- Moderate (section entrances, small hover interactions)
- Rich (heavier animation, parallax, immersive)

Default assumption is "subtle" if not answered. Note it as an assumption if so.

**4.7 Perceived quality and trust level**
On a spectrum from "approachable local business" to "premium professional firm" — where should this site sit? Is trust built through warmth and relatability, or through sophistication and restraint?

This should match the audience described in Batch 1. If there is a mismatch, flag it.

## Redesign Branch — Additional Required Questions

Only ask these if the project is a redesign.

**R.1 Current site URL**
What is the URL of the existing site?

**R.2 What is not working**
What is the primary problem with the current site? Conversion rate, visual quality, user experience, mobile experience, page speed, trust, unclear messaging, outdated look, or something else?

**R.3 What must be preserved**
Are there any elements of the current site that must be kept? This can include specific content, brand assets, page structure, or technical setup.

If they want to keep something that is also causing the problem they named in R.2: flag the contradiction directly.

**R.4 Migration constraints**
Are there domain, hosting, or CMS constraints? Is content already written and ready to migrate, or does it need to be rewritten?

**R.5 Previous conversion baseline**
If known: what was the previous site converting at? Calls per week, form fills, bookings? This sets a benchmark the new site should beat.
