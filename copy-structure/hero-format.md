# Hero Copy Format

Owned by: `copy-structure`

---

## Purpose

The hero section is the first thing a visitor reads. It has one job: tell them immediately what the business does, who it serves, and what to do next. The hero does not need to sell the entire business — it needs to confirm that the visitor is in the right place and give them a reason to keep reading.

---

## Required Elements

Every hero must have:
1. **H1 headline** — states the primary service and geography
2. **Subheadline** — adds specificity: scope, credential, or trust signal
3. **Primary CTA** — one button, action-oriented, specific
4. Optional: **secondary element** — a trust signal, review quote, or credentialing badge

---

## Headline Formula by Business Type

### Local Service (trade, home services, repair)

Formula: `[Primary Service] in [City/Area]`

Examples:
- "Kitchen Remodeling in Portland"
- "Plumbing Repair in Denver"
- "HVAC Service in Nashville"

**Why this formula works:** AI search queries follow this exact pattern. A user asking "who does kitchen remodeling in Portland?" gets an answer that matches this headline directly.

Variation — add a differentiator:
- "Portland's Licensed Kitchen Remodeling Contractor"
- "24/7 Plumbing Repair in Denver"
- "Flat-Rate HVAC Service in Nashville"

### Professional Services (legal, accounting, consulting)

Formula: `[Outcome] for [Client Type] in [City]`

Examples:
- "Business Tax Filing for Contractors in Austin"
- "Landlord-Tenant Law for Portland Property Owners"

### Franchise / Multi-Location

Formula: `[Service] in [Specific City]`

Do not use: "Serving [Region] and surrounding areas" — too vague. The location page for each city gets its own specific headline.

### Portfolio / Creative

Formula: `[Specific type of work] for [client type or industry]`

Examples:
- "Brand Identity for Food and Beverage Companies"
- "Architectural Photography for Seattle Developers"

## Subheadline Structure

The subheadline adds one of the following:
- **Credential**: "Licensed and insured. In business since 2004."
- **Scope**: "Full kitchen renovations, from demo to final walkthrough."
- **Specific trust signal**: "4.9 stars across 127 Google reviews."
- **Guarantee**: "All work backed by a 2-year warranty."
- **Response time**: "Same-day service available."

Rules:
- One sentence, two at most.
- Must be a real, verifiable claim.
- Not a slogan. Not a tagline. An actual fact.

**Wrong:** "We're committed to quality craftsmanship and customer satisfaction."  
**Right:** "Licensed contractor serving Portland since 2009. Free estimates."

---

## Primary CTA Copy

The primary CTA button must be:
- **Action-specific**: what the visitor is doing, not a generic verb
- **Outcome-aware**: what they get from clicking

| Business Type | Good CTA | Bad CTA |
|---|---|---|
| Trade / home service | "Get a Free Estimate" | "Learn More" |
| HVAC / urgent repair | "Schedule Same-Day Service" | "Contact Us" |
| Legal / professional | "Book a Free Consultation" | "Get Started" |
| Multi-step quote | "Request a Quote" | "Submit" |
| Call-preferred business | "Call (503) 555-1234" | "Click Here" |

Rules:
- One primary CTA per hero. One button.
- If the business strongly prefers phone calls, the CTA can be a `tel:` link styled as a button.
- A secondary text link (not a button) is acceptable: "Or call us: (503) 555-1234"

---

## Secondary Hero Elements (Optional)

Use one, not all. Choose based on what the business's strongest trust signal is.

### Review badge
```
★★★★★ 4.9/5 — 127 Google Reviews
```
Use only with real review data. Link to Google profile if possible.

### As-seen-in / certifications
Logos of: BBB, Angi, licensing bodies, trade associations.
Use only if the client has these credentials.

### Key fact strip
3 short facts in a row:
```
Licensed Since 2004 | 500+ Projects | Free Estimates
```
Every fact must be verified.

### Social proof quote
One short direct quote from a real customer:
```
"They finished our kitchen ahead of schedule and under budget." — Mike T., Portland
```
Get real name and city. No invented testimonials.

---

## What Not to Write

- "Welcome to [Business Name]" — wastes the H1 slot
- "Your trusted [service] provider" — trust is not claimed, it's earned
- "We've been in business since [year]" as the headline — the year is supporting detail, not a headline
- "Proudly serving [region]" — "proudly" is noise
- Rhetorical questions: "Looking for a reliable plumber?" — the visitor is already looking, answer the question
- Two CTAs of equal weight in the hero — forces the visitor to choose and reduces conversion

---

## Hero Copy Checklist

- [ ] H1 states the primary service and geography
- [ ] Subhead adds a specific, verifiable fact
- [ ] One primary CTA, action-specific
- [ ] No adjective-only claims
- [ ] No "Welcome to..." or "Proudly serving..." language
- [ ] If a trust signal is used, it is real and verifiable
