STRIPE DESIGN BREAKDOWN
Source: https://stripe.com/en-ca
Analyzed: 2026-04-05
==================================================

1. LAYOUT STRUCTURE
-------------------
Strict single-column progressive disclosure: brand promise → product proof → social proof → conversion.
No sidebars, no clutter. Each section has one job.

Section order:
  Nav → Hero → Logos → Products → Metrics → Case Studies → Developer → News → Footer CTA → Footer

Wide but constrained — content max-width is generous but well-padded. Nothing bleeds awkwardly.


2. HERO SECTION FORMULA
------------------------
Structure: Big bold claim + one-line clarification + dual CTA

- Headline: direct economic benefit ("grow your revenue"), not a feature list
- No fluff adjectives ("powerful," "seamless," "modern") — just the outcome
- Two CTAs with clear hierarchy: primary self-serve ("Get started") + low-friction social login ("Sign up with Google")
- Background is atmospheric, not literal — animated wave signals "tech" without being stock-photo generic

Formula to copy:
  [Outcome] + [Who it's for] → [CTA] / [Secondary CTA]


3. TYPOGRAPHY CHOICES
----------------------
- Custom sans-serif (Stripe uses their own typeface derived from Inter/system stacks for body)
- Weights: 700+ for headlines, 400–500 for body — very high contrast between levels
- Uppercase labels on CTAs and badges (scannable, authoritative)
- No decorative fonts — zero serif, zero display fonts, everything is functional
- Line heights are generous in body copy, tight in headlines


4. SPACING AND SECTION RHYTHM
------------------------------
- Generous padding — sections breathe; each section feels like its own landing page
- Consistent internal padding: content never touches the edge
- Rhythm alternates: wide full-bleed section → constrained text section → wide again
- Stats and metrics get their own dedicated "moment" — they don't compete with copy


5. CTA PLACEMENT
-----------------
Location              | CTA                              | Purpose
--------------------- | -------------------------------- | -------------------
Nav                   | "Start now" + "Contact sales"    | Always available
Hero                  | "Get started" + "Sign up with Google" | Capture intent
Mid-page case studies | "Read the story"                 | Soft engagement
Developer section     | "Read the docs"                  | Technical funnel
Footer                | Full CTA block                   | Last-chance conversion

Key pattern: CTAs are never far away, but never feel aggressive.
The footer CTA is a full dedicated section — not an afterthought.


6. TRUST-BUILDING ELEMENTS
---------------------------
- Hard stats with specificity: $1.9tn processed, 135+ currencies, 99.999% uptime — not vague claims
- Logo carousel with brands everyone recognizes (Shopify, Amazon, Anthropic, Uber)
- Case studies with metrics — not "they loved us" but "X% revenue increase for Y company"
- Direct quotes from named people at named companies
- Parallelogram-framed real photography of actual businesses using the product


7. WHAT MAKES IT FEEL PREMIUM (NOT AI-GENERATED)
--------------------------------------------------
- No stock photos — all imagery is custom illustration, custom photography, or real product UI
- Restraint in color — very few accent colors, only used with purpose
- Custom shapes — the parallelogram crop is a signature, immediately recognizable element
- Metrics are verifiable — real numbers, not percentages without context
- Copy doesn't try too hard — no superlatives, no exclamation points, just confident declarative sentences
- Micro-animations are subtle — the wave doesn't demand attention; it rewards it
- Section transitions are intentional — each section earns the next, creating a narrative arc


8. WHAT TO COPY FOR A CLIENT WEBSITE
--------------------------------------
- The hero formula: outcome-first headline, one supporting line, two-tier CTA
- Stats block: 3–4 hard numbers with large typography in their own dedicated section
- Logo carousel: even 6–8 recognizable logos dramatically increases credibility
- Case study structure: brand → metric → quote → "products used" tags
- CTA-free breathing room — several sections have no CTA; this builds trust before the ask
- Constrained color palette: 1 brand color, 1 neutral, white backgrounds, dark text
- Uppercase labels for category/tag elements


9. WHAT NOT TO COPY TOO LITERALLY
-----------------------------------
- The animated wave hero — so associated with Stripe it reads as a clone immediately
- The parallelogram image crop — their signature shape; avoid it directly
- The purple — Stripe's purple is culturally linked to them specifically
- The exact stat categories (uptime, currencies) — relevant to payments infra, not most client businesses
- The "backbone of global commerce" scale of messaging — works for Stripe because they genuinely are that;
  smaller businesses using this tone sound inauthentic


TL;DR
------
Confident, restrained, outcome-first copy + hard proof + breathing room + persistent but non-aggressive CTAs.
The premium feel comes from what they LEAVE OUT, not what they add in.
