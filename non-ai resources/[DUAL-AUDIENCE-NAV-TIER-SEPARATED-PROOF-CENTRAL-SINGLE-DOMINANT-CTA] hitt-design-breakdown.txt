HITT CONTRACTING DESIGN BREAKDOWN
Source: https://www.hitt.com/
Analyzed: 2026-04-16
==================================================

CONTEXT
--------
HITT is a national commercial construction firm with ~90 years of history, $13B projected
annual revenue, and 3,300+ employees across 16 offices. The site serves two distinct
high-value audiences simultaneously — potential clients (project owners, corporate real
estate decision-makers) and potential talent (experienced construction professionals).

This site is a strong reference for:
- Dual-intent homepage serving two audiences without fragmentation or audience selectors
- Keeping project proof central while routing two different journeys
- Navigation architecture as the primary audience-sorting mechanism
- Single dominant CTA discipline (one yellow button per page)
- Premium B2B/professional services positioning without corporate stiffness
- Trust through process transparency rather than testimonial walls


1. LAYOUT STRUCTURE
-------------------
Section order (top to bottom):

  Primary Navigation (mega-menu, client-forward)
  → Secondary Navigation (right-aligned: News, Locations, Contact, Subcontractors, Careers)
  → Hero ("Trust built through every experience")
  → Value Proposition Statement (1–2 sentences, brand promise)
  → Projects Filterable Gallery (center of page, serves both audiences)
  → Key Metrics Block (4 stats: $13B, 83% repeat, 3,300+ team, 16 offices)
  → Markets/Practices Grid (13 markets × 3 practices)
  → Experience Framework Section (5-phase client journey)
  → "You Belong Here" Talent Recruitment Block
  → R&D / Innovation Section ("Igniting Progress")
  → News/Insights Carousel (3 articles)
  → "Let's Chat" CTA Prompt (footer entry)
  → Footer (Work With HITT + Learn More columns)

Client-audience sections front-load the page. Talent section appears after all proof content.
This is not an accident — it communicates primary audience priority without labeling it.


2. DUAL-AUDIENCE MECHANISM — THE CORE PATTERN
----------------------------------------------
The site serves two audiences through SPATIAL SEPARATION + UNIFIED IDENTITY.
There is no audience toggle. No "Are you a client or job seeker?" prompt. No split screen.

The mechanism is:

| Layer | Client Path | Talent Path |
|---|---|---|
| Primary nav | Expertise (Markets, Practices), Projects, Experience | — |
| Secondary nav | News, Locations, Contact | Careers (co-equal item) |
| Hero language | "Trust built through every experience" | Same headline — applies to culture too |
| Page sequencing | Projects, metrics, markets come first | "You Belong Here" section comes after proof |
| CTA wording | "Build With Us" | "Join Our Team" |
| Proof type | Project portfolio, 83% repeat rate | 199 promotions in 2024, named contacts |

WHY IT DOESN'T FEEL SPLIT:
- The headline ("Trust built through every experience") is intentionally ambiguous — clients read
  trust in execution; talent reads trust in culture. One message, two valid interpretations.
- The projects section simultaneously demonstrates: capability (for clients) + breadth of work
  types and geographic reach (for job seekers evaluating career growth).
- "You Belong Here" comes after, not alongside. Sequencing signals priority; it doesn't conflict.

Formula to copy:
  [Shared value proposition in hero] → [Client proof first] → [Talent section after proof]
  Nav: [client-forward primary nav] + [careers in secondary nav as equal item]


3. HERO SECTION FORMULA
------------------------
Headline: "Trust built through *every* experience"
  — Declarative claim, not aspirational. Uses italic on single keyword for emphasis.
  — "Every" is the operative word — it's a process commitment, not a one-time promise.

Subhead: "Delivering a premium construction experience on every project we build."
  — Reinforces the headline, adds specificity (premium, every project).
  — "Premium experience" positions them away from commodity contractors.

Body copy:
  "We work tirelessly and intentionally to earn your trust by making every experience
   with HITT exceptional. From routine service work to complex corporate headquarters
   and data center campuses across the nation, our goal is to achieve success together.
   You have a vision for your next big project — work with HITT to make it a reality."
  — Scope range ("routine service" to "data center campuses") = both approachable and capable.
  — "Success together" = partnership framing, not vendor framing.
  — Ends with "You have a vision" — addresses the client directly.

CTA: "Build With Us"
  — Action verb + "with us" (partnership, not transaction).
  — Yellow #D6DB1D button — the ONLY yellow button on the homepage.

Background: Full-bleed high-res construction photography, overlay applied.
Animation: Scroll-triggered fade-in on hero elements (opacity 0 → 1).

Formula to copy (B2B professional services):
  [Process commitment as headline] + [Scope range as proof of breadth]
  + [Partnership-framed closing line] → [Action verb + "with us" CTA]

What this hero deliberately avoids:
  - No stats in the hero (stats have their own dedicated section)
  - No list of services above the fold
  - No "Get a free quote" language (too transactional for this positioning)
  - No auto-playing video
  - No carousel


4. NAVIGATION ARCHITECTURE
---------------------------
Primary nav (left, client-forward):
  Expertise (mega-menu)
  ├─ Markets (13 categories: Healthcare, Mission Critical, Life Sciences, etc.)
  └─ Practices (Core & Shell, Repositioning, Tenant Fit-Outs)
  Projects → /projects/
  The HITT Experience (client journey, safety, tools)
  R&D (vision, projects, insights, CoLab)
  Our Team (principal + senior leadership)
  About (who we are, corporate responsibility, You Belong Here, sustainability)

Secondary nav (right, organizational):
  News Hub | Locations | Contact | Subcontractors | Careers

Key design decision:
  Careers is in the secondary nav — visible and equal, but not competing with the primary
  client-facing structure. Talent users see it immediately; it doesn't distract clients
  who are scanning for Expertise or Projects.

Mega-menu on hover (desktop). Hamburger collapse under 768px.


5. PROJECT PROOF — CENTRAL TO BOTH AUDIENCES
---------------------------------------------
The projects filterable gallery sits at the center of the homepage — not below the fold,
not relegated to a "Our Work" section. It is the primary proof layer.

Filter axes:
  - 12+ market categories (Healthcare, Office, Mission Critical, etc.)
  - 15+ city/location filters
  - 3 practice types (Core & Shell, Renovations, Tenant Fit-Outs)

For clients: filtering demonstrates category specialization. A healthcare client filters
Healthcare and sees precedent and depth. Confidence builder without needing a call.

For talent: the breadth of filterable options signals geographic reach and type diversity —
evidence that career growth and varied work exist at the firm.

The gallery serves both audiences simultaneously through the same component.
No audience-specific gallery. One proof layer, two interpretations.

Project detail page structure (not narrative case studies):
  - Location, Client Name, Contract Value, Square Footage, Timeline
  - Project overview (scope description, not challenge-solution-result)
  - Key Team Members with photos and titles (executive visibility for talent)
  - Related Projects
  - "Let's Chat" CTA footer

Notable: No traditional "challenge → solution → result" case study format.
Instead: announcement/credential model. States what was built and for whom.
This works for high-confidentiality institutional clients who cannot be quoted.


6. TYPOGRAPHY
-------------
Font stack: System sans-serif (Inter/-apple-system/similar). Not a custom display font —
the work and trust signals carry the brand weight, not typography.

Size scale (conservative range, not extreme jumps):
  Page/section hero: 42–48px, weight 700
  Section subheads: 36px, weight 700
  Card titles: 18px, weight 700
  Body: 16px, weight 400
  Small/legal: 13px, weight 400
  Buttons: 14px, weight 500

Special treatments:
  - Italics used sparingly for single-keyword emphasis (not decorative)
  - Bold within paragraph text for highlights
  - No excessive letter-spacing — weight contrast does the hierarchy work
  - No decorative, serif, or display typeface

Key insight: modest size scale (13–42px). Weight does more work than size.
Italics are semantic, not stylistic. Typography is functional — gets out of the way.


7. COLOR SYSTEM
---------------
Yellow/Lime  #D6DB1D — Primary action color. Hero CTA button ONLY. Used once.
             Semantics: optimism, movement, "let's go."

Trust Blue   #1863DC — All secondary CTAs and exploration links throughout.
             Semantics: reliability, intelligence, "keep reading."

Black        #000000 — Headlines, body text. Authority, clarity.
White        #FFFFFF — Backgrounds, breathing room.
Dark Gray    #121212, #2A2A2A — Section backgrounds. Sophistication.
Medium Gray  #757575, #D0D0D0 — Footer links, secondary labels. Hierarchy.
Border Gray  #D0D5D2 — Dividers, structure.

Color discipline:
  Yellow button appears ONCE on the homepage (hero). This is extreme restraint.
  The scarcity makes it the most powerful element on the page.
  Every other CTA is a blue text link — exploratory, not committal.
  No red, orange, or aggressive colors. Avoids the commodity contractor aesthetic.


8. SPACING AND SECTION RHYTHM
------------------------------
CSS spacing preset system (design tokens approach):
  --spacing-20: 0.44rem (7px)   — tight internal spacing
  --spacing-40: 1rem (16px)     — standard internal
  --spacing-50: 1.5rem (24px)   — section internal
  --spacing-60: 2.25rem (36px)  — section separation
  --spacing-80: 5.06rem (81px)  — hero/feature section breathing

Rhythm pattern:
  Hero: BREATHES (80px+ padding) — one job: set tone
  Proof/projects: DENSE (50–60px) — information earns the density
  Metrics: DENSE — stat clarity requires focus
  Talent section: BREATHES (80px+) — emotional section needs space
  Footer CTA: TRANSITIONS

Button proportions: 8px vertical × 27px horizontal padding = taller, narrower = premium.
(Most templated sites use square padding. This elongated proportion reads as considered.)


9. CTA MAP
----------
Location              | CTA                   | Type          | Purpose
--------------------- | --------------------- | ------------- | -------------------
Primary nav           | (none)                | —             | Exploration only
Hero                  | "Build With Us"       | Yellow button | Primary conversion
Experience section    | "Trust, Built"        | Blue text     | Soft engagement
Projects section      | "View All"            | Text link     | Portfolio depth
Markets grid          | Category cards        | Card links    | Service exploration
Talent section        | "Join Our Team"       | Blue text     | Talent conversion
R&D section           | "See Our Spark"       | Blue text     | Innovation exploration
Footer entry          | "Let's chat."         | Large text    | Last-chance recovery
Footer nav            | "Careers," "Who We Are" | Text links  | Tertiary

CTA wording patterns:
  "Build With Us" — action verb + "with us" (partnership)
  "Join Our Team" — joining, not applying (community framing)
  "Let's chat." — lowercase, period. Conversational. Low-friction. Not "Contact Sales."
  "See Our Spark" — curiosity, not commitment

One yellow button. Multiple blue text links. Never more than 3 primary CTAs visible at once.


10. TRUST-BUILDING ELEMENTS
----------------------------
Quantified metrics (used once, in dedicated section):
  "$13 Billion Projected Annual Revenue"
  "83% Client Repeat Business" — retention as proof of satisfaction
  "3,300+ Team Members" — scale without impersonal feel
  "16 offices nationwide"

Institutional positioning claim: "National Leader in Commercial Construction"
Ranking: "#10 Top General Contractor" (Engineering News-Record) — cited factually, not flaunted

Process transparency (Experience page — 5-phase journey):
  Consideration → Award → Preconstruction → Construction → Post-construction
  Each phase describes HOW they operate, not WHAT they promise.
  "Available even pre-award" — credibility starts before the contract.
  This is systematic trust, not promise-based trust.

Talent-specific trust signals (Careers page):
  - 4 testimonials with full name, title, department, and direct email
  - Quotes address anxiety: "Relationships are what make HITT successful"
  - Named recruiting contact (Cory Hilliard, Recruiting Manager + email)
  — Named contact beats a generic form. It signals human-scale culture.

What trust-building deliberately avoids:
  - No client logo wall (institutional clients require confidentiality)
  - No generic "awards" section
  - No stock photography or AI imagery
  - No testimonials on client-facing pages (only careers page)
  - No "Since [year]" vanity heritage — trust is earned ongoing, not inherited


11. WHAT MAKES IT FEEL PREMIUM (NOT AI-GENERATED OR TEMPLATED)
---------------------------------------------------------------
- Single yellow CTA per page: extreme discipline. Template sites have CTAs everywhere.
  Scarcity paradoxically makes the one yellow button the most powerful element.
- Custom design token system: spacing, colors, and typography via CSS variables
  (signals intentional design system, not default theme settings)
- Named contacts instead of forms: "Cory Hilliard, Recruiting Manager + email"
- Metrics are verifiable and specific: "83% repeat" not "high retention"
- Process transparency: 5-phase journey describes method, not outcome
- Typography restraint: italics used for one word, not entire headlines
- No exclamation points in client-facing copy (only in testimonials)
- No "industry-leading" self-labeling anywhere
- No popups, chat widgets, or urgency language
- Section breathing: 80px+ padding for emotional/hero sections vs. 50px for dense proof
- High-res original construction photography throughout (no stock)
- Responsive architecture restructures at multiple breakpoints (845px, 768px, 576px, 425px, 352px)
  — not just font scaling, but layout changes

What it deliberately avoids:
  "Industry-leading" / "award-winning" / "best-in-class" anywhere
  Auto-playing video in hero
  Carousel for core content
  "Join the [adjective] team" clichés
  Gradient-heavy aesthetic
  Headshot testimonial wall
  Urgency language ("limited availability," "act now")
  Chat widgets


12. PATTERNS TO COPY FOR LOCAL SERVICE BUSINESSES
--------------------------------------------------
These patterns transfer directly to local service businesses (remodeling, painting, etc.):

Dual-audience without fragmentation:
  - If a business hires subcontractors or staff, don't create a separate "Jobs" page
    hidden in the footer. Give it a nav slot in secondary nav, then a "You Belong Here"
    section at the bottom of the homepage, after all client proof.
  - Hero language should work for both audiences — "Built on referrals" reads as quality
    to clients and culture to potential hires.

CTA scarcity:
  - One dominant button (primary color) per page.
  - All other navigation is text links (secondary color).
  - The one button cannot be confused with a link.

Process transparency as trust:
  - A 3–5 step "how we work" section builds more trust than 10 testimonials.
  - Describe the method, not the outcome. "We send a lead carpenter and a dedicated PM
    to every job" is more credible than "We deliver exceptional results."

Named contacts:
  - "Questions about hiring? Call Dana at (503) 847-2291" beats a generic contact form.
  - Even on small local sites, a named contact signals that real people are reachable.

Stats with specificity:
  - "63 kitchen projects in Portland since 2018" beats "hundreds of happy clients"
  - Specific numbers signal that they can be verified. Ranges signal that they were made up.
