MAMMUT EIGER EXTREME — EXPEDITION BAIKAL DESIGN BREAKDOWN
Source: https://eiger-extreme.mammut.com/en
Analyzed: 2026-04-05
==================================================

CONTEXT
--------
This is Mammut's campaign microsite for their Eiger Extreme collection, framed around
pro climber Dani Arnold's expedition to Lake Baikal. It is NOT a standard e-commerce page.
It is a long-form editorial/narrative microsite where the story earns the product sale.

This site is a strong reference for:
- Narrative-first product marketing ("story sells, then product")
- Scroll-driven product reveal within a story context
- How outdoor/adventure brands justify premium pricing through proof-of-use
- Hotspot overlays on photography as a product discovery mechanism
- Athlete-as-endorsement vs. celebrity-as-endorsement


1. LAYOUT STRUCTURE
-------------------
Modular, scrollable storytelling format. Sections alternate between:
  - Narrative/atmospheric (expedition story, conditions, athlete biography)
  - Product showcase (dedicated per-product blocks with specs + shop CTA)
  - Editorial collage (multi-image tiles, video embeds)

Section order:
  Blur/Animated Intro → Two-Image Hero Collage → Athlete Info → Environmental Metrics
  → Product 1 (Eigerjoch Pro Jacket) → Three-Column Image Tiles → Text Interstitial
  → Full-Width Vertical Image → Horizontal Product Slider → Product 2 (Nordwand Pro Jacket)
  → Product 3 (Helmet) → Product 4 (Boots) → Products Summary Grid → Next Section CTA

No sidebar. No nav menu cluttering the experience. Full-bleed sections throughout.
The scroll journey has a clear narrative arc: world → person → conditions → gear.


2. HERO SECTION FORMULA
------------------------
Structure: Atmospheric background + athlete cutout + location name + expedition stats

- Full-width landscape image (the environment is introduced before the person or product)
- Foreground cutout of the athlete layered over the background — creates depth
- Centered heading: "Baikal" — one word, no tagline, no product mention
- Expedition stats below: Depth (-5030m), Temperature (-40°C), Wind (up to 120 km/h)
- Product image embedded as a link in the subheading — product enters the story, not the hero

Formula to copy (for outdoor/adventure/performance brands):
  [Environment establishes stakes] → [Athlete in environment] → [Location as headline]
  → [Conditions as stats] → [Product enters as the solution, not the focus]

Critical insight: the hero does NOT show a product. The conditions ARE the product pitch.
By the time a product appears, you already understand why it exists.


3. TYPOGRAPHY CHOICES
----------------------
- Primary: Large, bold Helvetica-adjacent sans-serif — authoritative, not decorative
- Stats: Bold numerical format with units prominent — data as drama
  (depth, temperature, wind speed are displayed like instrument readings, not copy)
- Body copy: Medium weight, left-aligned — editorial/journalistic register
- Product names: H3 weight within showcase blocks — subordinate to the narrative
- Labels: Small weight for USP callouts and measurement units
- No expressive or editorial typeface — the photography and conditions carry the emotion;
  type remains functional and credible


4. SPACING AND SECTION RHYTHM
------------------------------
- Full-bleed sections throughout — no visible max-width constraint
- Generous vertical padding above and below each major content block
- The rhythm alternates: atmospheric/narrative (wide, slow) → product (structured, detailed)
  → atmospheric/narrative → product — like breathing in and out
- Product showcase blocks are denser than narrative blocks — intentional; you've been
  softened up by the story before the product asks for your attention
- Product photography is center-aligned with symmetric margins — isolation signals importance


5. PRODUCT DISPLAY — THE CORE FOCUS
-------------------------------------

## The Primary Product Showcase Pattern

Each major product gets a DEDICATED FULL BLOCK with this consistent structure:
  1. High-resolution portrait product photograph (approx. 1412×2206px)
     — isolated on neutral/white background, narrow width, extended height
     — focal point data embedded (x/y coordinates) — the image is always well-composed
  2. Gender toggle (Men/Women) — switches both photography and shop URL
  3. Product title as a linked heading (tapping = product page)
  4. 3–4 rotating USP slides (benefit carousel):
     - Material name ("Ultra-light Pertex® Quantum Pro")
     - Function description ("insulated with water-resistant filling")
     - Performance spec ("Fill power 850 in³ — warmest jacket Mammut offers")
  5. Vertical gauge/meter visualization showing an environmental metric (temperature, etc.)
  6. "Shop [Product Name]" CTA button — gender-specific URL

## How the USP Slides Work
Each slide auto-rotates through benefit statements while the product image stays fixed.
The product is always visible; the text changes around it.
This creates the illusion of the product being inspected from multiple angles while
physically only one image is shown — attention stays on the product, not the copy.
Zoom factor varies per product image (1.0–1.7) — subtle zoom on the static image
adds perceived motion without a carousel.

## Hotspot Overlay System
Hotspots are positioned absolutely over editorial/lifestyle photography using
x/y pixel coordinate data. A spot on a jacket in a lifestyle photo links directly
to that product page. The coordinates are baked into the image data — precise.

This means: products are "findable" inside the story photography, not just in
dedicated showcase blocks. A viewer watching the athlete climb can click on his jacket.

## Transition Triggers Between Products
- Scroll-based reveal (not hover, not click, not timed auto-advance)
- Products are separated by narrative/atmospheric sections — story acts as a palate cleanser
  between product reveals
- No scroll-jacking detected — native scroll is preserved, transitions happen on scroll entry
- Lazy loading per section — each product block loads as it enters the viewport
- Horizontal slider used for secondary products — scroll-triggered carousel

## Product Photography Approach
- Isolated product shots on neutral background (portrait orientation, no lifestyle context)
  used for the main showcase blocks — allows the product to be assessed clinically
- Lifestyle/action shots in editorial collages — emotional context
- The two types are never mixed in the same block: one shows what it looks like,
  the other shows why you need it
- Separate mobile/portrait-oriented image variants — the product photography is
  re-shot or re-cropped for mobile, not just scaled down

## Product Sequencing Strategy
The order products appear follows logical on-body layering from outside to inside:
  Shell jacket → insulation layer → helmet → boots
This mirrors how you'd actually dress for an extreme mountaineering expedition —
the site teaches the full system, not just individual items.


6. CTA PLACEMENT
-----------------
Location                    | CTA                          | Purpose
--------------------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------
Hero subheading             | Embedded product image link  | Soft product intro
Below each product block    | "Shop [Product Name]"        | Direct purchase
Products Summary Grid       | Gallery of all products      | Full collection view
End of page                 | "GORE-TEX PRO Technology"    | Brand/tech deep dive

Key pattern: CTAs only appear AFTER the product has been fully justified by the story.
"Shop" is earned, not demanded. There is no "Buy Now" before you've understood why.
The CTAs are product-specific ("Shop Eigerjoch Pro IN Hooded Jacket") — never generic.


7. TRUST-BUILDING ELEMENTS
---------------------------
- Athlete biography: Name + role + start year + birthdate — hyper-specific, not PR-fluffed
- Audio clips from the athlete ("A new project" — Dani Arnold) — voice as authenticity
- Real expedition conditions as design context: -40°C, -5030m, 120 km/h wind
  — these numbers validate every product claim before it's made
- Material certifications: GORE-TEX PRO, Pertex® Quantum Pro, fill power ratings (850 in³)
  — third-party material brands carry their own credibility (GORE-TEX is trusted globally)
- The expedition itself as proof-of-use: products weren't tested in a lab, they were
  used on a real climb, in real conditions, by a real professional

Trust approach: conditions → person → materials → proof-of-use
Never: "5-star review" or "trusted by thousands"


8. WHAT MAKES IT FEEL PREMIUM (NOT AI-GENERATED)
--------------------------------------------------
- Environmental stats displayed as instrument readings (not marketing claims)
- Athlete has a birth date, membership year, and professional designation — not a brand ambassador,
  a documented person with a documented career
- Product focal point coordinates are baked into the asset data — nothing is carelessly cropped
- Hotspot system over lifestyle photos — technical precision in linking products within narrative
- Gender toggle switches BOTH the image and the product URL — not just a label swap
- USP slides rotate while the product image stays fixed — the product never leaves the frame
- Products are introduced in the order you'd dress for the climb — editorial intelligence
- Mobile image variants are re-shot/re-cropped, not just scaled — production investment is visible
- The word "Baikal" is the hero headline — a place name with no explanation.
  The audience is trusted to be curious, not told to be impressed.


9. WHAT TO COPY FOR A CLIENT WEBSITE
--------------------------------------
- Conditions-first hero: establish the problem/environment before the product appears
  — works for any performance product (fitness, outdoor, medical, professional tools)
- USP rotating slide carousel with fixed product image: keeps attention on the product
  while delivering multiple benefit messages — very efficient use of vertical space
- Dedicated full block per hero product: don't list products in a grid;
  give each one its own moment with photography, benefits, and a CTA
- Hotspot overlays on lifestyle photography: let viewers discover products inside the story
- Product sequencing that follows real-world usage logic (how you'd use them, not SKU order)
- Material/certification brands as trust anchors (GORE-TEX, Pertex, etc.) — third-party
  ingredient brands work for any industry (certifications, ingredients, components)
- Athlete/expert biography with specific details (dates, years, role) not just a name + quote
- Audio/video testimonials from the person, not a written quote card
- Gender/variant toggle that switches both image and URL — clean, functional, no extra page load
- "Shop [Specific Product Name]" CTAs — never generic "Shop Now"


10. WHAT NOT TO COPY TOO LITERALLY
------------------------------------
- The single-expedition narrative structure — only works if the client has a genuinely
  compelling story and a real person willing to be documented
- Environmental condition stats as hero copy — specific to performance/outdoor brands;
  wrong for brands where conditions are not part of the value proposition
- The near-total absence of pricing above the fold — works for aspirational brands
  where desire is built before cost is considered; wrong for price-competitive clients
- The hotspot coordinate system — requires custom development or a platform (like Contentful
  or a headless CMS) that supports embedded coordinate data
- The "Baikal" one-word hero headline — works because the brand and athlete do the lifting;
  an unknown brand with a one-word headline reads as vague, not confident


TL;DR
------
Mammut's Eiger Extreme site is the definitive example of narrative-first product marketing.
The product is never the story — the conditions are. By the time a product appears,
the viewer already understands the stakes, trusts the person using it, and believes
the spec sheet is real because they've seen the environment that demanded it.

The product display technique (fixed image + rotating benefit slides + isolated portrait
photography + gender toggle + hotspot in lifestyle shots) is one of the most sophisticated
product presentation systems in consumer web design. Every element earns the viewer's trust
before the "Shop" CTA ever appears.

Core lesson: context sells products. Conditions sell performance products.
The site's job is to make the viewer feel the cold before they read the spec sheet.
