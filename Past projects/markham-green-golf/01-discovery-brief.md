- **Season opens:** April 24, 2026

---

## 2. Business Context

Markham Green is a public 9-hole golf course in Markham, Ontario, managed by PGA of Canada Professional Scott Haynes. The course offers affordable green fees for recreational golfers and hosts a professional instruction program run by Greg Salazar (Salazar Golf Academy), including private lessons, a Ladies Learn to Golf series, and junior camps. The facility also includes a food and beverage operation. The current website is sparse, lacks key operational information, and fails to drive any meaningful conversion. A full redesign is needed before the 2026 season opens.

---

## 3. Goals

- **Primary goal:** Drive tee time bookings — phone calls to the pro shop now, transition to online booking when the system is ready
- **Secondary goal:** Drive golf lesson and junior camp inquiries through the Greg Salazar program
- **Out of scope:** E-commerce, membership portal, blog, live scoring, tournament management, CMS

---

## 4. Target Audience

- **Primary:** Adult recreational golfers, approximately 25–60, living in Markham and York Region. Casual players — not competitive or club-level. They want an easy, affordable, pressure-free round of golf near home. They have likely played before but are not daily golfers.
- **Secondary:** Parents of juniors interested in camps and beginner instruction; women new to golf looking for a structured, welcoming entry point (Ladies Learn to Golf series).
- **Who they are NOT:** High-handicap beginners looking for an introductory experience at a driving range only. Serious tournament golfers who prioritize course difficulty and prestige. Private club members.

---

## 5. Customer Pain Points

1. **Other courses in the area are too expensive** — Angus Glen, Royal Woodbine, and similar courses charge $100+ per round. Recreational golfers can't justify that for a casual weekday game.
2. **Popular courses are hard to get on** — Booking windows at well-known courses fill quickly. Markham Green is accessible without weeks of advance planning.
3. **Formal golf clubs feel intimidating** — Dress code enforcement, etiquette pressure, and a culture of seriousness puts off casual players who just want to enjoy the game without judgment.

## 12. Visual Direction

**Overall feel:** Refined but welcoming. Dark greens and quality typography signal a proper golf club. Warm photography and approachable copy ensure it doesn't feel exclusive or intimidating. Premium without the "members only" coldness.

**Visual references:** None provided. Direction derived from answers.

**Extracted design principles:**
- Dark hunter green as primary background/accent colour — establishes credibility and golf context
- Near-black for depth on key sections (hero, trust bar) — quality signal without gold-and-marble excess
- Warm off-white or cream for body sections — prevents harshness; keeps it approachable
- Gold or warm amber as a single accent — used sparingly on CTAs or hover states only; not as a dominant colour
- Serif typeface for headings — classic golf club feel, establishes authority
- Clean sans-serif for body and UI — keeps it modern and readable on mobile
- Photography does the emotional work — wide course shots, natural light, people enjoying the game
- No stock imagery of generic golfers in polos — real course, real atmosphere

**Banned styles:**
- Pure private-club exclusivity signals (white glove, black tie imagery)
- Cheap sport-website defaults (neon colours, excessive gradients, stats-heavy dashboards)
- Generic WordPress golf themes (clip art flags, cartoon golf ball graphics)
- Cluttered layouts with too many competing CTAs

**Colour constraints:** No existing brand colours confirmed. Dark green (#1a3a2a range), near-black (#0f1f17), warm cream/off-white for surfaces. Single warm accent (gold/amber). Final tokens set in design system stage.

**Font constraints:** Serif for headings (Playfair Display, Cormorant, or equivalent). Clean geometric sans-serif for body (Inter or Neue Haas Grotesk equivalent). Final selection in design system stage.

**Animation level:** Subtle. Fade-in on scroll for content sections. No parallax. No looping elements.

**Quality register:** 7–8 out of 10 toward premium. Established club feel, but open to the public. Not intimidating.
